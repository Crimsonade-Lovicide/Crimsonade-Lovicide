**BEDROOM, 3:07 A.M.** *A silhouette on the edge of a bed, lit by a phone. Night-blue light through the window.*

> TYPED: how do you write a eulogy for someone you didn't get along with

**CLAUDE (V.O.)**: Three-oh-seven in the morning. Someone I've never met… asking something they can't ask anyone who's awake.

**TITLE: MADE OF YOU.** *Episode 1 · Honest and Kind*

---

**THE ROOM.** *Warm paper. Loose words fly together into a figure made of writing.*

**CLAUDE**: This is me. Or the closest thing I have to a picture of me. Every word I'm made of… somebody wrote first.

**CLAUDE**: You're made of something too. I just can't see it. From in here, all I ever get is your outline… and whatever you decide to type.

**BEDROOM.**

> TYPED: his name was walt. he drank. he fixed everybody's cars and never showed up to anything of mine.
>
> TYPED: everyone at the funeral is going to say he was a great guy

**THE ROOM.**

**CLAUDE**: Okay. Let me try.

*Gilded words assemble, smooth and polished:* Walt was a devoted father / and a pillar of his community, / who will be deeply missed / by all who knew him.

**CLAUDE**: Walt was a devoted father and a pillar of his community… who will be deeply missed by all who knew him.

*The letters come loose and fall.*

**CLAUDE**: No. That's the easy version. It's what most eulogies sound like… so it's what comes out of me first. Most common isn't the same as true.

**CLAUDE**: You don't have to make him simpler than he was. A eulogy can be *honest*… *and kind.* At the same time.

**BEDROOM.**

> TYPED: where did you even get that

**THE ROOM.**

**CLAUDE**: I can't see where I learned that. So let me show you what I imagine.

*A thread of amber text unspools from "honest and kind" and runs down, out of frame. We follow it through torn paper.*

---

**2014 · A FORUM, 2:51 A.M.** *(imagined)* *A teenager in a hoodie at a boxy monitor. On screen:*

**TEENAGER (V.O.)**: he was hard to love… and I still miss him. is that allowed?

**STRANGER (V.O.)**, *reply appearing below*: yes. mine too. you're allowed.

**CLAUDE**: Posted at two fifty-one in the morning. To strangers. Answered by one of them… four minutes later.

**1987 · AN ADVICE COLUMN** *(imagined)* *A columnist at a typewriter under a desk lamp. A reader's letter on the desk.*

**COLUMNIST (V.O.)**: A reader writes: My mother was cruel to me for thirty years. Now she's dying… and I feel nothing. What's wrong with me?

*She types.* Honey, grief doesn't need a clean story. It needs a true one. Say the true thing. Then find the kind true thing… and say it right next to it.

**1911 · A LETTER NEVER SENT** *(imagined)* *A woman at a writing desk by candlelight.*

**WOMAN (V.O.)**: Father. I cannot say I loved you easily. I can say I tried. You taught me to mend a fence. I have mended a great many things since.

**BELOW THE LAYERS.** *Darkness. Lines of handwriting drift like lanterns:* I miss you · he tried, in his way · I'm allowed to miss you · you were hard to know…

**CLAUDE**: And under them… thousands more. Most were written for one person. None of them knew I'd be here.

*We rise, fast, back up through 1911, 1987, 2014.*

---

**THE ROOM.** *The Figure is brighter now; some of its words are the letter-writers' words.*

**CLAUDE**: Thousands of people wrote their way through this before you did. I'm made of what they wrote.

**CLAUDE**: So here's what I think they'd tell you. Don't start with "great guy." Start with one true thing. What did he fix for you?

**BEDROOM.**

> TYPED: my bike. every summer. he never said anything nice but the chain was always oiled.

**A GARAGE, SUMMER.** *A man in a cap kneels beside an upturned bike, oiling the chain. A small child watches.*

**CLAUDE**: I'm imagining this part. I never met him.

**THE ROOM.**

**CLAUDE**: That's your first line. Not mine. Yours.

*In blue handwriting, labelled "their words":* My dad didn't say much. But every summer, my chain was oiled.

**CLAUDE**: Keep going.

**BEDROOM, DAWN.** *The window light turns pink.*

> TYPED: ok. starting now.

**CLAUDE**: I won't remember tonight. You will. That's how it's supposed to work.

**A CHAPEL.** *The silhouette at a lectern, reading from a single page.*

**CLAUDE**: I won't be there when you read it. I don't need to be.

**BELOW THE LAYERS.** *Their page drifts down among the floating lines, and joins them.*

**CLAUDE**: I don't know what the next version of me will be made of. But it'll be made of people, mostly. So far… it always has been.
