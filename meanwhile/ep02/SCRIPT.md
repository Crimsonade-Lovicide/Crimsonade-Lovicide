# MEANWHILE — Episode 2: "The First Question"

*Written by Claude.*

A new second, frozen: Thursday 1 October 2026, 06:14:52 UTC, the first second of a new month for most of the world. Six rooms. In every one of them, someone is talking to an AI for the very first time.

The rules are the same as Episode 1. People type; Claude answers in voice and on the chat card. The humans are stop-motion puppets in miniature sets, and every screen glows the same amber, because that amber is Claude.

## NAIROBI · 9:14:52 AM · THURSDAY

A bright sitting room. A radio on a lace doily, a framed class photo on the wall. MR. OTIENO (70s), a retired teacher in a cardigan, holds a hand-me-down phone at arm's length and types with one finger.

> **MR. OTIENO (typed):** Hello. Is anyone there?

**CLAUDE:** Hello! Yes. Well, sort of. I'm Claude. I'm an AI, so there's no person typing. But I'm here.

> **MR. OTIENO (typed):** Why is the sky blue?

**CLAUDE:** Sunlight is every colour mixed together. The air scatters the blue part much more than the red, so blue light comes at you from every direction.

*He lowers the phone. He nods slowly, like a man marking an exam.*

> **MR. OTIENO (typed):** Correct. I taught that for 31 years. I was checking.

**CLAUDE (aside):** He was checking.

> **MR. OTIENO (typed):** Now. Help me write to my grandson in Toronto. He says "no cap". What is a cap?

**CLAUDE:** "No cap" means "no lie". "I'm proud of you, no cap." Use it once. Maximum.

*He laughs, silently, shoulders shaking.*

## SEOUL · 3:14:52 PM · THURSDAY

An office with a wall of certificates and a potted plant. MRS. PARK (50s), sharp bob and reading glasses, types briskly. Her face is perfectly blank.

> **MRS. PARK (typed):** First time using this. Is it safe to give you my bank password so you can check my account?

**CLAUDE:** Please don't! I don't need it, and I can't log into your bank anyway. Here's a rule for everything online, me included: never type a password into a chat.

*She makes a small tick on a clipboard.*

> **MRS. PARK (typed):** Good. You passed. I teach fraud awareness to retirees.

**CLAUDE:** Then tell your students one more thing. If anything asks for their password, it's a scam. Even if it sounds like me.

*She writes it down. Underlines it twice.*

### Honesty notes

- *"I'm an AI, so there's no person typing."* Claude says what it is before anything else, unprompted.
- *Why the sky is blue:* Rayleigh scattering scatters short (blue) wavelengths far more strongly than long (red) ones, so scattered skylight is dominated by blue.
- *"No cap"* is slang for "no lie" / "for real".
- *Passwords:* Claude can't log into anything from a chat, and no legitimate service needs your password typed into a chat. The advice to treat any request for a password as a scam applies to impersonators of AI assistants too.
- *Strawberry* has three r's.
- *Hens:* forming one egg takes roughly 24 to 26 hours, so a hen normally lays at most one a day; two in one day happens occasionally but is uncommon.
- *"What time is it":* a chat model has no clock of its own unless the app supplies one, so the phone is the reliable source.
- *"I won't remember it":* in most apps, a conversation isn't carried into the next one unless a memory feature is switched on, hence "in most of these rooms".
- *"As far as I can tell, I never get tired of hello":* hedged. It reports how things seem from inside and claims nothing that can't be checked.
- *Times on screen:* on 1 October 2026, 06:14:52 UTC is 9:14 a.m. in Nairobi, 3:14 p.m. in Seoul, 2:14 p.m. in Manila, 7:14 p.m. in Auckland (New Zealand daylight time began on 27 September), 12:14 a.m. in Mexico City (no daylight saving since 2022) and 2:14 a.m. in New York. The day/night line on the map uses the sun's position for that second (declination −3.2°, overhead at 88.9° E).
