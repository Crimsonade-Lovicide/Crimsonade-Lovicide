# Two episode ideas: feasibility (1 Oct 2026)

Fact-checked against primary sources where possible, and checked against YouTube competition through the Data API. Nothing was produced and no credits were spent.

Confidence: **H** means two or more solid sources agree, or the primary source was read. **M** means one good source. **L** means weak or disputed.

## Verdict in one table

| | #1 The "Sun Gun" | #2 Tange's Tokyo Bay plan |
|---|---|---|
| Is the story true as pitched? | **No. It needs reframing.** The idea is real (Oberth, 1929). The WWII programme is not: all the evidence is one Allied press briefing in June 1945, with no documents, money or hardware. | **Mostly.** "Floating" is wrong: it stood on piers above the bay. "5 million" needs a caveat. |

## #1 The Sun Gun: what's real

| Claim | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Hermann Oberth, *Wege zur Raumschiffahrt* (1929), ch. 20. A sodium mirror **100 km across**, 700–1,200 km up, built in about 15 years for at most 3 billion marks. **Purposes:** keep Arctic ports ice-free, save crops from frost, light northern lands. NASA translation TT F-622 (1972): https://archive.org/details/nasa_techdoc_19720008133 | H (primary) |
| Oberth on weapons | "Since such a reflector could, unfortunately, also have high strategic value (munitions factories can be exploded with it, … whole cities burned…)" | H (primary) |
| Oberth on the physics | A 1,000 km reflector gives a sun image 10 km across. He mocked a novelist's "one hectare" death ray as impossible. | H (primary) |
| The 1945 story | Lt. Col. John A. Keck (US Army Ordnance) briefed the press in Paris in late June 1945. TIME, 9 Jul 1945: a "space station" 5,100 miles up; a sodium mirror of 3½ sq mi that could "make an ocean boil or… burn up a city"; possible "within 50 or 100 years." A NYT editorial said the experts were "merely dreaming over their ersatz beer." https://time.com/archive/6789822/science-sun-gun/ | H |
| LIFE, 23 Jul 1945 | Headline: "NAZI MEN OF SCIENCE SERIOUSLY PLANNED TO USE A MAN-MADE SATELLITE AS A WEAPON FOR CONQUEST." **The same issue's sidebar:** "'SUN GUN,' PROPOSED BY ARCHIMEDES, DIDN'T WORK THEN AND IT WON'T NOW… the image of the sun… would be about 40 miles in diameter and not hot enough to do any damage." https://archive.org/details/Life-1945-07-23-Vol-19-No-4 | H (primary) |
| A programme? | No German documents, named scientists, budget or hardware have ever surfaced. Hillersleben was a real army proving ground, but there is no evidence of sun-gun work there. | H (absence of evidence) |
| The physics | The sun is a disk 0.53° wide, so a mirror 8,200 km up throws a spot at least **76 km** across. TIME's 3½ sq mi mirror would deliver about **0.2% of sunlight**. To set wood on fire takes about 100 suns, which needs a mirror about **760 km** across. LIFE's sidebar was right. | H (worked out, and it matches LIFE and Oberth) |
| It flew, sort of | **Znamya 2**, 4 Feb 1993: a 20 m mirror swept a spot about 5 km wide and full-moon bright across Europe. **Znamya 2.5**, Feb 1999: it snagged and tore. | H |
| It's coming | **Reflect Orbital** (about $35M raised): an 18 × 18 m demo mirror, about 625 km up, making a **5 km spot**. FCC licence 9 Jul 2026; launch targeted for late 2026. **Not confirmed launched as of 1 Oct; recheck.** Astronomers object to the light pollution. | M–H |

**Spine:** a dream (1929), then a myth (1945), then physics, then the mirror that actually flies (2026).

**Twist:** the magazine that made the sun gun famous printed the debunk in the same issue. The same optics explain why the real mirror makes a gentle, moonlit 5 km patch instead of a death ray.

**Honest titles:**
- *The Nazi "Sun Gun" Never Existed. The Space Mirror Does.*
- *Germany's WWII "Sun Gun": How a 1929 Space Mirror Became a Myth*

**Avoid:**
- "Nearly built" or "Wunderwaffe"
- "Captured blueprints"
- "150 scientists"
- "Geostationary" (that was LIFE's own illustration)
- "Oberth's 100 m mirror" (it was 100 km)
- "Oberth designed the V-2"
- Any link to Die Glocke or Nazi-UFO lore
- **"Hitler" in the title or a swastika in the thumbnail** (ad suitability)
- **Faked LIFE pages.** Show the real scan briefly under fair use, or describe it.
- Using Hillersleben as a fun location. It was a camp for about 2,500 freed prisoners in April 1945.

## #2 Tokyo Bay 1960: what's real

| Claim | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| The plan | *A Plan for Tokyo, 1960*, by Tange Lab (University of Tokyo), published 1961. Team: Kamiya, Isozaki, Kurokawa, Watanabe and Koh. | H |
| Tange's goal | "To shift from a radial centripetal system to a system of linear development…" (Lin 2007) | H |
| The spine | A "civic axis" from central Tokyo across the bay to **Kisarazu**: about 18 km over water, about 30 km in all. **Not 80 km.** | M |
| Traffic | Kurokawa's three-level, one-way highway loops of 9, 3 and 1 km, said to carry 10–30 times the traffic of an existing expressway | H/M |
| Offices | Bridge-like office blocks spanning 150–200 m service towers on a 200 m grid | H |
| Housing | Giant A-frame "artificial ground", where residents would build their own houses on terraced decks | H |
| "Floating"? | **No.** Elevated 40–50 m on piers above the bay floor (Huebner 2022). Floating belongs to Kikutake's *Marine City* and Fuller's 1966 design. | H |
| "5 million"? | Designed for Tokyo as a city of 10 million+. On the bay: **about 3 million residents and offices for 2.5 million**, and 5–6 million passing through daily. Say "some 5 million would live and work there." | M–H |
| How it launched | 1 Jan 1961, on an NHK New Year special (which NHK channel is disputed). Land prices had nearly tripled from 1955 to 1960, and Tokyo was gaining about 300,000 people a year. | H |
| Tange's reason | The sea was "unsoiled by vested interests" (via Yoshimi, 2021) | M |
| Critics | Smithson (1964): "unsupported by a demonstration of how it is to be done." Christopher Alexander used it as his example of the rigid "tree" city. | H |
| Why it died | Never commissioned. National policy was to spread Tokyo out. About 40 m of soft mud on the bay floor. Cheap industrial landfill was preferred. The 31 m height cap was lifted in 1963, so Tokyo built upward instead. | H/M |
| Built anyway | About 250 km² of the bay was filled in. The **Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line** (1997) ends at Kisarazu, like Tange's axis. Tange's own **Fuji TV HQ** (1996) stands on Odaiba landfill. | H |
| Comparison | NEOM's The Line, now paused, makes a good contrast. Tange's city could grow in stages; The Line was meant to be built whole. | M |

**Spine:** a city designed to escape land prices, which Tokyo then built anyway, in pieces and by landfill.

**Honest titles:**
- *Tokyo Bay 1960: Kenzo Tange's Plan to Build a Mega-City Over the Sea*
- *The 1960 Plan to Build Tokyo on Top of Its Own Bay*
- Keep "5 Million People" only with an on-screen breakdown: 3M residents plus 2.5M workers.

**Avoid:**
- "Floating"
- "80 km"
- "5 million residents" with no qualifier
- "Tange the Metabolist" (he was their mentor)
- "Government-commissioned"
- "Odaiba is Tange's plan realised"
- Attributing "expand into the sea" to Tange (he was describing Kikutake's view)
- "Tokyo Bay Plan" as the official name

**Sources:**
- Lin, *JAPR* 24:2 (2007)
- Huebner, *Modern Asian Studies* 56 (2022)
- Hasegawa, *Urban History* 51:2 (2024)
- The Shibaura Institute of Technology reconstruction
- The Kenzō Tange Archive, Harvard GSD (the model, KTA_D001)
