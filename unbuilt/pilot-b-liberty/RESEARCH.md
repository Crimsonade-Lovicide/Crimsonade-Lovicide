# Pilot B research: was the Statue of Liberty built for Egypt? (5 Oct 2026)

This builds on the myths-special research (`../special-myths/RESEARCH.md`, section "The Statue of Liberty was designed for Egypt"). It adds what a whole episode needs.

**Confidence ratings, as in Pilot A:**
- **H**: a primary source, or two solid sources that agree.
- **M**: one good secondary source, or sources that differ slightly. Script wording hedges every M.
- **L**: weak or disputed. L facts are kept out of the script.

## 1. Bartholdi and Egypt

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Bartholdi was born in Colmar, Alsace, on 2 Aug 1834, and died in 1904. | H | [NPS1]; Wikipedia |
| In Oct 1855, aged 21, he went on a four-month trip up the Nile with the Orientalist painters Jean-Léon Gérôme and Léon Belly. | H | [Aramco]; [NPS1] (the trip, without the date) |
| On the colossal statues, he wrote that "these granite beings, in their imperturbable majesty, seem to be still listening to the most remote antiquity". | M | [NPS1] quotes it as his. The original letter isn't identified. |
| In 1867 he went to see the Khedive Isma'il, who was in Paris for the Universal Exposition. | M | [Aramco] |
| In 1869 he spent several months in Egypt, as part of the French delegation for the opening of the Suez Canal. | M/H | [Aramco]; [NPS1] (the meeting and the pitch) |

## 2. The Egypt statue

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The title was "Egypt (or Progress) Carrying the Light to Asia". | H | [NPS1]; [Aramco] |
| It was a colossal lighthouse in the form of "an Egyptian fellah, a female serf" (NPS), "holding aloft a torch" (Aramco), meant for the entrance to the canal at Port Said. | H | [NPS1]; [Aramco] |
| The figure was to be 86 ft (26 m) on a 48 ft (15 m) pedestal. | H | Barry Moreno, via Smithsonian (myths research) |
| Bartholdi made clay or terracotta models and watercolours, now at the Musée Bartholdi, Colmar. The 1869 watercolour is public domain and on Commons. | H (watercolour) / M (models) | [Commons]; [Aramco] ("clay models") |
| **Why it died: money.** "A lack of funds stopped him" (NPS). The Khedive "did not have the financial resources needed to carry it out" (Aramco). | H | [NPS1]; [Aramco] |
| That it was de Lesseps who gave the warning. | M | Myths research, from [Aramco]. The script says "according to one account". |
| The Egypt figure, 86 ft, against Liberty's copper figure, 151 ft: **about 57%**, "a little over half". | H (arithmetic) | [NPS2] |

|---|---|---|

## 4. Liberty

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Liberty first appears in Bartholdi's papers only in Dec 1869, and he travelled to America in 1871. | M (Dec 1869) / H (1871) | [Aramco]; Wikipedia |
| Édouard de Laboulaye, an abolitionist and scholar of American democracy, framed the idea as a gift between two democratic countries. | H | [NPS1] |
| Within three months in America, Bartholdi chose Bedloe's Island in New York Harbor. | H | [NPS1] |
| It is copper (3/32 in thick) over an iron frame engineered by Gustave Eiffel, and was built in Paris. | H | [NPS2]; NPS; standard |
| From the top of the base to the torch it measures 151 ft 1 in (46.05 m); from the ground to the tip of the torch, 305 ft 1 in. | H | [NPS2] |
| It was dedicated on 28 Oct 1886. | H | NPS; standard |

## 5. The 1886 rumour, and its rebuttal (the spine)

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A syndicated paragraph ran in US papers in the month of the unveiling: "It has even been charged that M Bartholdi modeled the goddess originally to represent Commerce, for the purpose of setting it up at one of the entrances of the Suez canal as a light-house, but that Ismail Pasha did not like the design, and that it was palmed off as a goddess breathing the spirit of freedom. It is a fact that with the advice of Count de Lesseps M Bartholdi did make a sketch of a figure of Egypt with the face of a female fellah, but the sketch does not at all resemble the one now on Bedloe's island." | H (primary) | [LoC], read in the Library of Congress OCR. **Check the scan before it goes on screen.** These are the newspaper's words, not Bartholdi's. |
| Bartholdi's "both held a light aloft" denial. | **L** | Traced on 2 Oct and not found (myths research). **Not used anywhere, including the description.** |
| "Muslim woman": the word comes from the Smithsonian.com headline of 2015. The article itself says "fellah, or Arab peasant". I found no 19th-century source that uses it. | H (headline) / M (absence) | [Smith]; myths research. The script says "I couldn't find it", not "it doesn't exist". |

## 6. The twist: the statue that was built at the canal

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A statue of Ferdinand de Lesseps was unveiled at the harbour entrance of Port Said on 17 Nov 1899. | H | Several (samdurant.net "Defaced monuments"; Lonely Planet "Stone plinth"; Egypt Independent) |
| It was pulled down in 1956, during the Suez Crisis. | H | same |
| The empty pedestal still stands at the harbour. | M/H | Lonely Planet; samdurant.net |
| The statue survives, restored, in a shipyard garden at Port Fouad, across the canal. | M | samdurant.net; Egypt Independent; a 2020 plan to put it back drew debate (Yahoo/AFP). The script hedges this. |
| Port Said to Liberty Island is about 9,000 km, or **5,600 miles**, great-circle. | H (arithmetic) | Computed from the coordinates |
