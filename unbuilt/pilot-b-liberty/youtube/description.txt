In 1869, Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi pitched Egypt a colossal lighthouse: a robed peasant woman holding a light over the entrance to the Suez Canal. Seventeen years later he unveiled the Statue of Liberty. So was Liberty built for Egypt? People were asking that the month she was unveiled, and the newspapers that spread the rumour also printed the best evidence against it.

This is the story of "Egypt Carrying the Light to Asia": the Nile trip that gave Bartholdi his obsession with colossal statues, the pitch to the Khedive Isma'il, why it was never built, how the idea crossed an ocean, where the "originally a Muslim woman" version comes from, and the one statue that was put up at the mouth of the canal.
