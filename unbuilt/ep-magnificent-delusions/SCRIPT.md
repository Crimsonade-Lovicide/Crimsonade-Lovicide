## No. 5 · THE ELEPHANT OF THE BASTILLE · Paris, 1808 (0:40–1:50)

**[E1] SYNC** · walking across the Place de la Bastille by day
> In 1808, Napoleon decided this square needed a fountain. Being Napoleon… he meant an elephant.

**[E2] VO** · architect's watercolour elevation, slow push
> Twenty-four metres, tower and all. To be cast in bronze from cannon captured at the Battle of Friedland. A staircase up one leg, a lookout on its back, and water from the trunk.

**[E3] VO** · 1814 reconstruction: workmen finishing a full-size plaster elephant on timber scaffolding
> First, a full-size model in plaster, to see how it looked. That was 1814. The following year was Waterloo.

**[E4 / E4b] VO** · the plaster elephant 15 years on: grey, cracked, fenced off, in the rain. Then a rat on the plinth.
> The bronze never came. The plaster stayed, for thirty years. It cracked, it sagged, and by the late 1820s the neighbours were petitioning to have it removed. Because of the rats.

**[E5] VO** · a small door in the elephant's leg, lamplight inside
> A watchman is said to have lived in one of its legs. Which is not a career they mention at school.

**[E6 / E6b] VO** · night; a ragged boy slips through a gap in the elephant's belly; candle glow inside
> Then Victor Hugo moved a tenant in. In *Les Misérables*, the street boy Gavroche sleeps inside the elephant. "Ugly in the eyes of the bourgeois," Hugo wrote. "Melancholy in the eyes of the thinker."

**[E7] SYNC** · MCU, the July Column behind him
> They knocked it down in 1846 and put up *that*. The July Column. Tasteful. Correct. Nobody's ever written a novel about it.

## No. 4 · BEACH PNEUMATIC TRANSIT · New York, 1870 (1:50–3:05)

**[B1] VO** · 1869 Broadway gridlock, hand-tinted archive look
> New York, 1869. Broadway is so jammed with horses and omnibuses that walking is faster.

**[B2] SYNC** · Broadway at Warren Street today
> Alfred Ely Beach, publisher of *Scientific American*, had permission to build a little tube for the mail. So, naturally… he built a train.

**[B3] VO** · night, lamplight, a round iron tunnelling shield under the street
> Working out of the basement of a clothing shop, his men dug under Broadway for fifty-eight nights.

**[B4 / B4b] VO** · the cylindrical car in the round brick tunnel; then the enormous blower
> February 1870. One car, twenty-two seats, blown down the tunnel by a fan the size of a locomotive… and then sucked back again.

**[B5] VO** · the waiting room: frescoes, grand piano, goldfish fountain, bright lamps
> The waiting room had frescoes, a grand piano and a fountain full of goldfish. Frankly, nicer than most stations in New York today.

**[B6] VO** · queue of Victorian passengers at the ticket window
> A ride cost a quarter, and the money went to an orphans' charity. Four hundred thousand people rode it in the first year.

**[B7] VO** · Albany, a governor's desk, a pen, a document pushed aside
> To go any further, Beach needed the state's permission. Unfortunately, the state belonged to Boss Tweed. And Tweed's governor, John T. Hoffman, vetoed the bill. Twice.

**[B8a / B8] VO** · 1873 bank-run crowds; then 1912, lanterns, workmen breaking into the old tunnel and finding a rotted wooden car
> Then the crash of 1873 scared off every investor in America. The tunnel was sealed and forgotten, until 1912, when men digging a new subway line broke straight into it… and found Beach's car. Still waiting.

**[B9] SYNC** · subway stairs near City Hall
> New York's first real subway opened in 1904. Beach beat it by thirty-four years. The reward for being that early, it turns out… is being forgotten.

## No. 1 · ATLANTROPA · The Mediterranean, 1928 (5:15–6:40)

**[A1] SYNC** · on the Rock of Gibraltar, wind, the strait behind
> And so, number one. In 1928, a Munich architect called Herman Sörgel looked at the Mediterranean Sea… and decided there was rather too much of it.

**[A2] VO** · 1930s-style illustrated map, dams drawn across the sea
> His plan: a dam across the Strait of Gibraltar, thirty-five kilometres long. More dams at the Dardanelles, and between Sicily and Tunisia. Then let evaporation do the work.

**[A3 / A3b] VO** · the colossal dam, sea visibly lower on the Mediterranean side; then exposed seabed turned to farmland, a stranded ship
> The western Mediterranean would fall by a hundred metres, the eastern by two hundred. Over half a million square kilometres of new land. Hydroelectric power for half of Europe.

**[A4] VO** · Venice, its lagoon drained to mud, a new canal cut to a distant sea
> Venice would need a canal to reach the sea. Most other ports would simply be left high and dry.

**[A5] VO** · map of Africa overdrawn with European plans and flooded basins
> And Africa? In Sörgel's plans, it was a blank space to be flooded, farmed and run for Europe. Nobody thought to ask the people who lived there.

**[A6] VO** · 1930s lecture hall, lantern-slide projection of the dam, the speaker seen from behind
> He sold it for a quarter of a century: lectures, films, exhibitions, even a symphony. He believed one great shared project would stop Europe going to war again. Europe went to war again.

**[A7] VO** · an empty snowy Munich road at dusk, a bicycle on its side
> On Christmas Day 1952, cycling to give a lecture, Sörgel was hit by a car and killed. The Atlantropa Institute closed in 1960.

**[A8] SYNC** · Gibraltar at sunset
> The maddest idea on this list. And the only one that would have redrawn the map of the world. Sörgel wanted to lower the sea. These days… we'd settle for holding it back.

### Script notes

- **Easter egg (fact):** the governor who vetoed Beach's subway twice (1871 and 1872) really was **John T. Hoffman**. I left it deadpan and uncommented. It's your call whether Hugo adds "no relation".
- **Hedged on purpose:** the watchman in the leg is "said to have lived" (single-sourced). The cathedral is "billed as" the second-largest church. Sörgel's death is stated plainly; the murder theory is one 2006 magazine piece, so it's left out.
- **Myths deliberately avoided:** the Beach tunnel was *not* secret (Beach publicised it). The elephant was *never* bronze. The piano was *not* found in 1912. The Beach tunnel is *not* the famous abandoned City Hall loop station.
