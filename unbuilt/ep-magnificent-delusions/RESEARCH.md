# Research & fact-check — "Magnificent Delusions"

Confidence levels: **H** means two or more solid sources agree. **M** means one good source, or sources that differ slightly. **L** means weak or disputed. Every number in the script is H or M, and M items are worded with a hedge.

## Why these five (and what was ruled out)

**Already covered by UNBUILT, so excluded.** These come from the Higgsfield production history for 14–26 Sep 2026:
- ep01 Mile High Illinois; ep02 The Line; ep03 Volkshalle; ep04 Palace of the Soviets; ep05 The World; ep06 Tatlin's Tower.
- ep07 Key Project; Hotel Attraction (test only); ep08 Plan Voisin; ep09 Sky City; ep11 Watkin's Tower; ep12 The Mukaab.
- Raymond Hood's Hudson apartment bridges. This episode also covers **Lindenthal's North River Bridge** and Ammann/GWB.
- Leonardo's Golden Horn Bridge (in production on 26 Sep).
- Also skipped: Willson's pyramid on Primrose Hill (an unused test exists, so it may be earmarked).

**Selection criteria:**
1. A story with a turn, not just a big render.
2. Variety of type: monument, subway, church, city-scale dome, sea-scale megaproject.
3. Escalating scale, so the countdown gets bigger each time.
4. Room for a dry host.
5. Something physical left to stand in or near (the crypt, the Bastille basin, the Biosphère).

## No. 5: Elephant of the Bastille (Paris, 1808–1846)

- Napoleon's fountain monument. First stone laid 2 Dec 1808. **H**
- 24 m in total including the pedestal and howdah; the animal itself was about 15 m. **M**
- The decree of 24 Feb 1811 said it was to be cast from guns captured at **Friedland** (1807). It was never cast. **H**
- Staircase inside one leg to a platform on its back; water from the trunk. **H**
- Full-size model in plaster over a timber and iron frame, by Bridan, finished 1814. The architect was Alavoine from 1812. **H**
- Stood about 30 years. From the late 1820s neighbours complained of rats. **H**
- A watchman (Levasseur) is said to have lived in a leg. **M**, single-sourced, so the script says "said to have".
- Demolished 1846. **H**
- *Les Misérables* (1862), Vol. 4 Book 6: Gavroche lives inside it. Hugo's line is "ugly in the eyes of the bourgeois, melancholy in the eyes of the thinker." **H**
- The July Column (1835–40) now stands there, and the elephant's basin survives in its base. **H/M**

**Avoid:** "made of bronze"; "thousands of rats poured out on demolition" (legend); "24 m animal".

## No. 4: Beach Pneumatic Transit (New York, 1869–1873)

- Alfred Ely Beach, publisher of *Scientific American*. His 1868 charter was for small mail and parcel tubes. **H**
- Tunnel about 300 ft long under Broadway, Warren St to Murray St; diameter 8–9 ft. **M**
- Built over 58 nights. Opened 26 Feb 1870. About $350k of Beach's own money. **H**
- Roots blower driven by a 100 hp steam engine, reversed to suck the car back. The blower's weight is given as 48–50 t. **H/M**
- Waiting room: frescoes, grand piano, fountain or goldfish, easy chairs, zircon lamps. **M/H**
- One 22-seat car. 25¢ fare, with proceeds to an orphans' home. About 400,000 riders in year one. **H**
- Governor **John T. Hoffman** (Tammany) vetoed extension bills in **1871 and 1872**. **H**
- In 1873 Governor Dix signed the bill, but the Panic of 1873 killed the financing. **H/M**
- In 1912 BMT workers broke into the tunnel and found the shield and the wooden remains of the car. **H**

**Avoid:**
- "Built in secret" (Beach publicised it).
- "Piano found in 1912."
- "Part of the abandoned City Hall loop station."
- "Rotting upholstery."

Sources:
- https://www.americanheritage.com/alfred-ely-beach-and-his-wonderful-pneumatic-underground-railway
- https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/nyunderground-secret-subway/
- https://www.nycsubway.org/wiki/Beach_Pneumatic_Transit
- https://www.mcny.org/story/beach-pneumatic-transit-company-just-bunch-hot-air

## No. 3: Lutyens' Liverpool Metropolitan Cathedral (1929–1958)

- Commissioned by Archbishop Downey in 1929. Billed as "the second largest church in the world after St Peter's". **M**, so the script says "billed as".
- Dome 168 ft (51 m) across, against St Peter's 137.7 ft. **H**
- Dome height over 500 ft. **M**
- Foundation stone laid Whit Monday, 5 June 1933. **H**
- Lutyens estimated 200 years to build. **M/H**
- Cost estimate £3m (1933) rising to £27m (1953). **H**
- WWII halted work in 1941. The crypt was completed in 1958, and it is the only part built. **H**
- Gibberd's cathedral was built 1962–67 on top ("Paddy's Wigwam"). **H**
- Model by J.B. Thorp, 1932–34, about 12 ft high. Restored 1992–2005 (13 years). Now at the Museum of Liverpool. **M/H**

**Avoid:** "twice the height of St Paul's"; a restoration-cost figure (none found).

- https://liverpoolmetrocathedral.org.uk/the-second-cathedral/

## No. 2: Dome over Manhattan (Fuller & Sadao, c. 1960)

- 2 miles in diameter. **H**
- Coverage river to river, about 21st to 64th St. **M**
- The snow-removal savings would pay for the dome in 10 years. **M/H**, widely quoted.
- 16 Sikorsky helicopters, 3 months, $200m. **M**, quoted from Fuller's 1960 text.
- It was never a commission: no client, no permit. **M**, inference, and worded that way.
- Expo 67 US Pavilion by Fuller & Sadao, now the Montreal Biosphère, still stands. **H**

**Avoid:** "one mile high"; "4,000 tons" (single weak source).

## No. 1: Atlantropa (Herman Sörgel, 1928–1952)

- Presented in 1928. Gibraltar dam about 35 km, sited west of the narrowest point. **M**
- Further dams: Sicily–Tunisia and the Dardanelles, plus works at Suez. **H**
- Sea level: western basin down about 100 m, eastern down about 200 m. **H**
- New land over 500,000 km² (sources give 500,000–660,000). **M**
- About 50 GW from Gibraltar; "half of Europe's electricity". **M**
- Congo dams to create a "Congo Sea" and "Chad Sea". **H**
- Venice would need a canal or dykes. **M**
- Promoted through lectures, films, exhibitions and an "Atlantropa symphony". **M**
- Pacifist framing: a shared project to prevent war. **H**
- Critique: Africa treated as empty land for Europe. **H**
- Died 25 Dec 1952 in Munich, hit by a car while cycling to a lecture. **H**
- The Institute closed in 1960. The archive is at the Deutsches Museum. **H**

**Avoid:** the murder theory (one 2006 magazine piece, **L**); "373,000 sq mi".

Sources:

- https://www.environmentandsociety.org/arcadia/atlantropa-endless-energy-mediterranean-sea
- https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantropa

## Stinger: Adolf Loos, Chicago Tribune column (1922)

- Competition for the Tribune's 75th anniversary. $100k in total prizes, more than 260 entries. The winners were Howells & Hood. **H**
- Loos's entry was a 21-storey black granite Doric column on a cubic base. **M**
- Whether it was satire or sincere is a genuine scholarly dispute: Rykwert says serious, Gravagnuolo says ironic, Masheck says "a dare". **H**
