# Research & fact-check: Special No. 3, Frank Lloyd Wright countdown

Researched 29 Sep 2026. Confidence: **H** means two or more solid sources agree, or a primary text was read directly. **M** means one good source, or sources differ slightly. **L** means weak or disputed; L claims are not used in the script.

Wright was born 8 June 1867 and died 9 April 1959, aged **91** (not 92). He long claimed an 1869 birth year, which is why some period press gives him two years younger.

---

## 1. The lineup and why

**The spine is scale.** Each entry is bigger than the last: a mountain, a riverfront, an island, a country, then a mile straight up.

| No. | Project | Date (Wright's age) | Scale | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | Gordon Strong Automobile Objective, Sugarloaf Mountain, MD | 1924–25 (57–58) | a building on a mountaintop, 150-ft planetarium dome | **The strongest opener.** The payoff lands in the first line: the Guggenheim's spiral was first drawn for cars. It has the best exchange of letters on the list (the Tower of Babel and the snail). |
| **4** | Point Park Civic Center, Pittsburgh | 1947–48 (79–81) | a drum a fifth of a mile across, 4½ miles of ramp | Wright had said Pittsburgh would be "cheaper to abandon", then drew its centrepiece. The Fallingwater client paid for it. |
| **3** | Plan for Greater Baghdad | 1957–58 (89–91) | an island in the Tigris, an opera, a university, a 300-ft statue | The most exotic images: a golden Aladdin on the roof, "Mother of Pigs" island renamed Edena. **Played mostly straight** (see cautions). |
| **2** | Broadacre City | 1932–58 (64–90) | a whole country, "an acre to the family" | The argument against the city. It sets up No. 1 as its opposite. |
| **1** | The Mile-High Illinois, Chicago | 1956 (89) | 5,280 ft | The famous one, the thumbnail, and the title. |

**Why not Wright plus a Russian architect.** The Palace of the Soviets (Iofan) was already covered as ep04, and the channel data shows no Russian-heritage effect (88% of viewers are in the US). Wright alone has more than five strong unbuilt projects, and his name is the searchable term.

**Overlap with the standard episodes.** The standard series already did the Mile-High (ep01, the channel's best long video, 902 views) and Ellis Island's Key Project (ep07). The Key Project is left out. The Mile-High stays at No. 1, because it's the one people search for. The segment is angled differently (the publicity stunt and the contradiction with Broadacre), and the end screen points to the full episode.

**Dropped:**
- **Crystal Heights**, Washington DC, 1940. It is used as the stinger instead (section 7).
- **The Oasis Arizona State Capitol**, 1957. Very quotable, but it is a second government-building rejection after Pittsburgh.
- **The Huntington Hartford Play Resort**, Hollywood, 1947. Good: 600 angry neighbours, and the site is now Runyon Canyon dog park. It is the best substitute if one entry falls through.
- **The Masieri Memorial**, Venice. Hemingway's "burn it" line has no primary source.
- **The Butterfly Bridge**, San Francisco. Charming but no turn.
- **San Marcos in the Desert.** The story is tragic rather than funny.

## 2. No. 5: Gordon Strong Automobile Objective (1924–25)

Main sources:
- Mark Reinberger, "Architecture in Motion", Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation (full text read; it quotes the letters at length): https://franklloydwright.org/architecture-in-motion-the-gordon-strong-automobile-objective/ (from Reinberger, *JSAH* 43:1, 1984).
- Library of Congress, "Designs for an American Landscape": https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/flw/flw02.html
- Guggenheim archive transcript of an Arthur Drexler talk, 1961: https://www.guggenheim.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/9009669_01_edited-Architecture-of-the-Guggenheim-Museum.pdf

| Claim | Figure / wording | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Client | Gordon Strong (1869–1954), a wealthy Chicago businessman, who had been buying land on Sugarloaf since 1902 | H |
| Commission | Summer 1924; the programme was fixed by Sept 1924. Presentation Aug 1925. | H |
| The brief | "an objective for short motor trips", mainly from Washington and Baltimore, with "the element of thrill, as well as the element of beauty" | H |
| Ramps | "automobiles would ascend clockwise, be reversed by a ramp at the top, and descend by counterclockwise ramp underneath the ascending one". Passengers could step out at the top. | H |
| Dome | A planetarium dome **150 ft** across. This is the only firm dimension. | H |
| First US planetarium | The Adler, Chicago, opened 12 May 1930, five years after the design | H |
| Strong's rejection (autumn 1925; exact date unknown) | "It looks to me as it were designed to be used anywhere in the United States – on any sloping hill or mere raise of ground." He enclosed a picture of the Tower of Babel and wrote: "Which was the end of the first attempt at an externally ramped automobile observatory." | H (wording) |
| Wright's reply (20 Oct 1925) | "I have found it hard to look a snail in the face since I stole the idea of his house – from his back." | H |
| What Strong did | Rejected it and kept a conventional park | H |
| Guggenheim link | Drexler (MoMA), 1961: "The Guggenheim Museum is the Gordon Strong project turned inside out." The Guggenheim was designed from 1943 and opened 21 Oct 1959. | H |
| Sugarloaf today | Owned by Stronghold, Inc. (1946), open to the public free | H |

**Avoid:**
- Any overall height, diameter or ramp length.
- Calling it a garage: the final scheme had no internal parking.
- "A Bruegel painting": he sent *a picture* of Babel.
- The V. C. Morris shop as the Guggenheim's prototype: Morris was 1948, and the Guggenheim design started in 1943.

## 3. No. 4: Point Park Civic Center, Pittsburgh (1947–48)

Main sources:
- Wikipedia, which cites Richard Cleary (*JSAH* 52:2, 1993; *Merchant Prince and Master Builder*, 1999) and Alberts, *The Shaping of the Point* (1980): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Park_Civic_Center
- TIME, 15 July 1935 (read directly): https://time.com/archive/6896106/people-jul-15-1935/
- Historic Pittsburgh / Heinz History Center photo captions.
- Carnegie Magazine, 1999.
- Pittsburgh Magazine, "'Burgh Snubs".

| Claim | Figure / wording | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| "It would be cheaper to abandon it!" | Late June 1935, answering newsmen who asked what could be done about Pittsburgh. Reported by the Post-Gazette on 1 July 1935 and TIME on 15 July 1935. | H |
| Patron | Edgar J. Kaufmann Sr. (Kaufmann's store; Fallingwater), who chaired the Allegheny Conference's Point Park committee | H |
| Dates | Talks began mid-1945; drawings were done by Apr 1947 (Scheme I); Scheme II was presented 4 Jan 1948; Kaufmann told Wright to stop by **30 June 1948** | M |
| Nickname | "Pittsburgh Point Park Coney Island in Automobile Scale" | H |
| Scheme I size | **One-fifth of a mile** across (about 1,056 ft), 175 ft high | H / M |
| Ramp | A two-way "Grand Auto Ramp" **4½ miles** long | H |
| Programme | Opera house, sports arena, three cinemas, convention hall and planetarium. A pavilion at the Point held an aquarium with specimens in glass spheres. A 500-ft light tower. | H (tower M) |
| Cost question | At a meeting, officials asked about cost and Wright said he did not care (Alberts) | M: script says "reportedly" |
| Scheme II | A tower of about **1,000 ft** (the "Bastion") with **two cable-stayed bridges** in prestressed concrete | H (height M) |
| Costs | Wallace Richards, relaying Wright: Scheme II "not less than $150,000,000", Scheme I "in the neighborhood of $400,000,000" | M: script says "by his own reported estimate" |
| Built instead | Gateway Center (1952), Fort Pitt Bridge (1959), Point State Park and fountain (dedicated 30 Aug 1974) | H |

**Avoid:**
- "Abandon it" as said at the presentation: it is from 1935.
- A fixed level count (13 vs 10).
- Any capacity figure (they conflict).
- Crediting the fountain to Wright.
- Confusing Scheme II with the separate "Point View" apartments (1951–53).

## 4. No. 3: Plan for Greater Baghdad (1957–58)

Main sources:
- Wright's own submission to the Development Board, as printed in a 1958 magazine (almost certainly *Architectural Forum*): https://loriemerson.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/wright-baghdad-plans.pdf. Primary; read directly.
- Opera magazine (J. Allison, Jan 2008).
- WNYC archive audio, July 1957: https://wnyc.org/story/44467-frank-lloyd-wrights-plan-for-iraq/
- Guggenheim 2009 press kit.
- Nature Iraq island survey.
- Robert Twombly (via Wikipedia).

| Claim | Figure / wording | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Who invited him | Iraq's Development Board, which controlled about 70% of the oil revenue, under King Faisal II | H |
| Visit | May 1957. He was 89, a month short of 90. | H (month) |
| Other architects | Le Corbusier (gymnasium), Gropius/TAC (university), Aalto (museum), Ponti (Development Board HQ), Dudok | H |
| Site | He rejected the site offered and, after audiences with the King, got permission to use an island in the Tigris owned by the King | M–H |
| Island name | Umm al-Khanazir, "Mother of Pigs" (after wild boar). Wright named it the "Isle of Edena". | H |
| Opera | "Grand Opera and Civic Auditorium": **1,600 seats plus 3,700 more** behind sliding screens. A crescent arch with *Arabian Nights* scenes. | H |
| Roof figure | "a golden figure of Aladdin and his wonderful lamp, the symbol of human imagination" | H |
| Harun al-Rashid | "a 300-foot-high statue of Haroun Al-Rashid" at the far end of the island | H |
| University | Drawn on the Jadiriya site, where TAC held the commission. Wright: "These drawings are voluntarily submitted to the Development Board." | H |
| Best primary quote | "…Nor need Baghdad adopt the materialistic structures called 'modern' now barging in from the West upon the East." | H |
| Radio, July 1957 | "…a cultural center for the place where civilization was invented — that is, Iraq." | H |
| Childhood | "When I was a youngster *The Arabian Nights* were my favorite stories and Baghdad was the center of them." | M: script uses "he said" |
| 14 July 1958 | The monarchy was overthrown; King Faisal II (23), the Crown Prince and members of the royal household were killed | H |
| New government | Objected that "the people needed food, clothing and shelter more than floating gardens, gold fountains, and a mammoth zoo" (Twombly) | M |
| Island today | Joined to the west bank, access restricted, used by police; "not really an island" (Nature Iraq, 2009–11) | M |

**Avoid:**
- "Opera for 7,000".
- "Cradle of civilization" as Wright's words: he said "where civilization was invented".
- "The island, Mr. Wright, is yours!" and "spotted it from the plane": both untraceable.
- "King Faisal commissioned him personally."
- Wright's claim that Harun al-Rashid built the round city: it was al-Mansur, in 762.

**Tone:** no jokes after the coup, and nothing violent on screen. The new government's case gets a fair hearing.

## 5. No. 2: Broadacre City (1932–58)

Main sources:
- *The Disappearing City* (1932), primary, read directly: https://archive.org/details/frank-lloyd-wright-the-disappearing-city
- *When Democracy Builds* (1945), primary, read directly.
- FLW Foundation (Jennifer Gray, 2018): https://franklloydwright.org/reading-broadacre/
- MoMA, "FLW and the City: Density vs. Dispersal" (2014).
- Aaron Betsky, *Metropolis*, 2017.

| Claim | Figure / wording | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| 1932 lines | "The big city is no longer modern." "To look at the plan of any great city is to look at the cross section of some fibrous tumor." | H (primary) |
| Rule, 1932 | "a minimum of an acre to the family" | H (primary) |
| Rule, 1945 | "an acre to the individual" | H (primary) |
| Model | About 12 ft square (MoMA: 152 × 152 in). It shows 4 square miles and 1,400 families. About 21 apprentices built it in Arizona, winter 1934–35. | H |
| Show | Opened 15 Apr 1935 at the Industrial Arts Exposition, Rockefeller Center | H |
| Panel | "THE FUTURE IS EVERYWHERE OR NOWHERE" | H |
| Legacy | "For decades, Wright was seen as the prophet of America's post-World War II suburban sprawl" (Betsky) | H as the common reading |
| Model today | MoMA (since 2012); shown 2014 and 2017 | H |

**Avoid:**
- "The city is doomed" and the "fail to recognize its coming" quote: neither is in the 1932 or 1945 texts.
- Helicopter "aerotors" on the 1935 model: they belong to the 1958 drawings.
- Saying Wright "caused" sprawl.

## 6. No. 1: The Mile-High Illinois (1956)

Main sources:
- Blair Kamin, Chicago Tribune, 28 May 2017 (Barry Bergdoll interview).
- Steiner's transcription of the Oct 1956 exhibition insert: http://www.steinerag.com/flw/Books/sixty.htm
- MoMA / Artbook reproduction of the Chicago Sunday Tribune of 26 Aug 1956.
- WBEZ, 2011.
- Architectural Record, 2010.
- The Daily Beast (Fortune).

| Claim | Figure / wording | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Scoop | Chicago Sunday Tribune, 26 Aug 1956 (then 510 storeys): "The Empire State Building would be a mouse by comparison." | H |
| Unveiling | Tue **16 Oct 1956**, Hotel Sherman, Chicago | H |
| Drawing | "22-foot", from period press. Another source says "more than 25 ft". | M: script uses 22 |
| Wright Day | Mayor Richard J. Daley proclaimed **17 Oct 1956** Frank Lloyd Wright Day | H |
| Specs (Wright's claims) | 528 storeys, 5,280 ft, 100,000 people, 15,000 cars, 100 helicopters (two decks of 50), 76 elevators, "Tandem-Cab, Ratchet Elevator Service, Atomic Power", with cabs five storeys tall | H |
| Wright on the contradiction | "If we're going to have centralization, why not quit fooling around and have it…" | H (Tribune 1956) |
| Why unbuilt | No client, no confirmed site, no budget | H |
| Elevators | Consultant James Fortune: "He wasn't even close. He should've had two hundred and fifteen to two hundred and twenty-five elevators." | M |
| Burj Khalifa | 828 m / 2,717 ft. Adrian Smith and Bill Baker **deny** the Mile-High was the inspiration (Architectural Record, 2010). Critics note the resemblance (Rybczynski, Slate, 2010). | H |
| Tallest in Sept 2026 | Still the Burj Khalifa. Jeddah Tower is about 454 m and under construction, target 2028. | M |

**Avoid:**
- "Burj's architect was inspired by it."
- A height including the antenna (sources conflict).
- "Wright was 87" or "died 1958".
- 130,000 occupants: that is analysis, not Wright's figure.
- A lakefront site as fact.

Main sources:
- Ghosts of DC, May 2026: https://ghostsofdc.org/2026/05/25/frank-lloyd-wright-crystal-heights/
- Wikipedia, "Crystal Heights".
- Library of Congress, Harris & Ewing photos.

| Claim | Figure / wording | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Unveiled | 25 Sept 1940, for developer Roy S. Thurman, on the 10-acre Temple Heights site. Glass, bronze and marble towers, a hotel, a theatre and parking for 1,500 cars. | M–H |
| Quote | "Versailles won't look like much compared to this when it is finished." | M–H |
| Killed | DC's height limits and zoning. The towers were cut to about 135 ft and still refused, Jan 1941. | M |
| Site today | The Washington Hilton. Not used: it is where Reagan was shot, and a joke there would land near a victim. | H |
