# UNHISTORY: series guide (set 14 Oct 2026)

**Decided by the owner on 14 Oct 2026:**
- The name is UNHISTORY.
- It runs as a named series on the UNBUILT channel first, not as a new channel.
- Real photos only.

Also read `../WORKING_AGREEMENT.md` and `../90_DAY_TEST.md`. UNHISTORY episodes count toward UNBUILT's one long video a week.

## What it is

Each episode answers one question: what would the world look like if one major event had gone the other way?

Most "what if" channels make the alternate world up. UNHISTORY builds it from the evidence: documents, plans and data that really existed. Where the evidence runs out, the narrator says so.

## The four rules

1. **Change as little as possible.** Pick one turning point that really could have gone the other way, with a documented reason. Historians call this the "minimal rewrite rule". Examples: a meeting that ended differently, or a decision that was argued over at the time.
2. **Label every stretch on screen** with one of two tags (set by the owner on 15 Oct):
   - **HISTORY**: what really happened, rated H or M in the episode's RESEARCH.md. The tag shows for about 3 seconds each time we come back to the real world, not on every shot.
   - **UNHISTORY**: our timeline. The tag stays up for the whole stretch, and the picture gets a dashed red border.
   - Every UNHISTORY beat is built on a real document, date or figure, shown first as HISTORY. Where the evidence runs out, the narration says so.
   - Aim for about 40% of the running time in UNHISTORY. The title promises a "what if", so the episode has to spend real time there.
3. **Admit where it stops.** Confidence falls fast with time. Each episode sets an end year, and past it the narrator says "from here, nobody knows."
4. **Real pictures only.** The real timeline gets archival photos. The alternate world appears only through:
   - real plans and documents, and photos of them;
   - charts and maps drawn by the toolkit, such as a dashed line splitting from the real one.

   There are no AI images. On 14 Oct the owner asked whether Higgsfield generations would help. Claude advised against them, for the reasons below, and the owner has the final say. The owner narrates in their own voice, so uploads can set "Altered or synthetic content: No".

## The look

- The same toolkit and paper palette as UNBUILT, with one addition: the HISTORY / UNHISTORY tag, top left, and the dashed red border on UNHISTORY shots.
- **Logo (option C, chosen by the owner on 15 Oct):** a red "UN" and ink "HISTORY" in the UNBUILT serif, over a timeline that splits into a dashed red branch. "an UNBUILT series" sits under it. Same typeface and paper as UNBUILT for the family link, but its own mark.
- Titles start with "What If…", because that's what people search for. Each title also carries the episode's own angle, so it doesn't copy the many "what if" videos already out there.

## Why no Higgsfield generations (Claude's advice, 14 Oct; opinion, confidence M–H)

1. **The angle is "their own plans".** AI pictures of a Nazi-won world are exactly what other "what if" videos do, so they would erase what sets this show apart.
2. **Made-up WWII photos spread as fakes.** A generated "1950 Germania" frame clipped out of context becomes misinformation with our name on it. That is a real harm, not just a brand risk.
3. **Platform and model risk.** Generated Nazi imagery is the content most likely to be age-restricted or flagged. Generators also often refuse it or distort it.
4. **The channel moved away from AI on purpose.** The owner judged the AI format to have no future (`../FORMAT_REPORT_2026-10.md`), and the AI backlash is a recorded risk.
5. **Credits:** the owner wants to spend none.

**What would change this:** if the pilots' retention shows real-photo episodes losing viewers early. Even then, test it first on a light subject (Hindenburg, Chicago 1871), never on Nazi or Holocaust material.

## Subjects about war, genocide or disaster

- **The context goes in the video itself.** It has to be in the narration and on screen, not only in the description: YouTube judges educational context from the images and audio. Source: YouTube's violent or graphic content and hate speech policies.
- **No swastikas or other extremist symbols in thumbnails.** Avoid them in the edit too, unless a document itself carries one and it is shown for a reason. Thumbnails fall under the same policies.
- **The victims are never the joke,** and the tone is never "cool". Plans for mass murder are described plainly, from the documents.

## Before each episode: research and stop rules

Each episode needs:
- a RESEARCH.md with H/M/L ratings, as in the pilots;
- a check of existing "what if" videos on the same subject, so the angle is new;
- a stop rule agreed in advance: what result would mean the series should change or stop. See the episode OUTLINE.md.
