## Story beats

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:30).**
   - The Germania model: a dome big enough for about 180,000 people, due by 1950. "This is real. The model is real. And the plans for the rest of the world survive too."
   - The question: "So we don't have to imagine what would have happened if Germany had won. They wrote it down. But first: could they have won at all?"
2. **They couldn't win a world war (about 1 min, HAPPENED).**
   - Harrison's GDP ratios as a chart: 2.4:1, falling to 2:1 in 1941, rising to 3.3:1 by 1944. US output nearly doubled.
   - The East was the only close front.
   - Conclusion: if Germany could win anything, it was a short war in Europe only.
3. **The door: May 1940 (about 2 min, HAPPENED, with the rewrite tagged GUESS).**
   - 9–10 May: the King and Chamberlain want Halifax. Halifax rules himself out.
   - 25–28 May: Dunkirk. Halifax argues to explore terms through Italy, and on 28 May the War Cabinet turns him down.
   - The rewrite: Halifax says yes on 10 May.
4. **A peace in 1940 (about 1.5 min, LIKELY or GUESS).**
   - No western front. Everything goes east in 1941. America has less reason, and no British base, to fight.
   - Say plainly that historians disagree about the East even in the real war. We don't pretend to know.
5. **The world they planned (about 3 min, HAPPENED: the documents).** In order, getting closer to home:
   - **The East:** the 2 May 1941 minutes, "tens of millions" to starve. Generalplan Ost: about 31 million people to be removed.
   - **Europe's Jews:** the Wannsee list, "over 11 million". It includes England (330,000) and Ireland (4,000), countries Germany never held.
   - **Britain:** the Black Book, about 2,820 names, Churchill among them.
   - **Berlin:** Germania, due by 1950.
   - The tone is plain and from the documents. No music swells.
6. **The stop line (about 20 s).** "1950 is as far as the evidence goes. After that, nobody knows, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing."
7. **What actually happened (about 1 min, HAPPENED).**
   - The real ending: building stopped in 1943, and Germania was never built.
   - What's left is the Schwerbelastungskörper, a 12,650-tonne concrete block poured in 1941 to test the ground. It sank three times further than allowed. It still stands in a Berlin street.
8. **Close.** "The plan was real. The only thing missing was the victory." Then the UNHISTORY / UNBUILT end card, with sources in the description.

The episode is sombre, which makes it a heavy start for the series. Later episodes can be lighter: Hindenburg, Chicago 1871.
