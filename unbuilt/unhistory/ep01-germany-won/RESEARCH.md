# UNHISTORY 01 research: what if Germany won WWII? (14 Oct 2026, first pass)

The owner chose this topic on 14 Oct 2026.

**Ratings, as in the pilots:**
- **H**: a primary source, or two solid sources that agree.
- **M**: one good secondary source, or sources that differ slightly. The script hedges every M.
- **L**: weak or disputed. L stays out.

The on-screen tags are separate:
- **HAPPENED**: real events and real documents.
- **LIKELY** and **GUESS**: the alternate timeline.

A plan rated H is a real document, so it gets HAPPENED for "they planned this." Whether it would have been carried out is LIKELY at most.

## The angle (and why)

Alternate History Hub has covered this topic at least six times:
- a four-part "What if Germany Won World War II?" (2016);
- "The Only Way Germany Could've Won" (2024), its sequel "America Strikes Back", and "War for the Atlantic" (2025).

Other channels have covered it too. An imagined Nazi world (Fatherland, The Man in the High Castle) is the most overdone what-if there is.

**Our angle:** we don't imagine the world they would have built. They wrote it down, and the documents and models survive. The episode does two things:
1. It shows, from the economics, why Germany could not win a world war, and finds the one door where a *European* victory was plausible: May 1940.
2. It builds the "won" world only from the regime's own plans, and stops in 1950, the year Berlin was meant to be finished.

## 1. Could Germany win a world war at all?

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Measured in prewar (1938) GDP, the Allied great powers of 1942 outweighed the Axis by 2.4:1. That fell to 2:1 in 1941, the Allied low point, then rose to 3.3:1 by 1944. | H | Harrison, *The Economics of World War II* (CUP 1998), ch. 1 overview: https://warwick.ac.uk/markharrison/public/ww2overview1998.pdf |
| By 1944, US GDP stood at nearly twice its 1938 level. | H | Harrison 1998, ch. 1 |

## 2. The door: May 1940

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| On 9 May 1940, Chamberlain, Halifax, Churchill and the Chief Whip, Margesson, met to choose a successor. Halifax ruled himself out, citing his seat in the Lords. Accounts of the meeting differ, and no record was taken. | H (the meeting) / M (exact words) | en.wikipedia "1940 British war cabinet crisis" and its cited diaries; National Archives blog, "10 May 1940" |
| Chamberlain recommended Halifax to the King. The King wished to appoint Halifax but was persuaded to choose Churchill. | H | Britannica, "George VI"; Wikipedia, "Edward Wood, 1st Earl of Halifax" |
| From 25 to 28 May 1940, as the army was trapped at Dunkirk, Halifax argued in the War Cabinet for exploring peace terms through Italian mediation. On 28 May, Chamberlain, Attlee and Greenwood sided with Churchill against it. | H | War Cabinet minutes (CAB 65/13; get the exact reference); Lukacs, *Five Days in London* (1999) |
| Churchill's "lies choking in his own blood" line to the wider cabinet on 28 May, from Hugh Dalton's diary. | M (not yet checked against the diary) | Dalton diary; quoted in Lukacs. **Check before use.** |
| The rewrite: Halifax accepts on 10 May, and later in May Britain explores terms. | GUESS (the minimal change; both halves were on the table) | — |
| Whether a Halifax government would really have signed a peace. | GUESS. Halifax wanted to *explore* terms, not to surrender. Say so. | Lukacs; Roberts, *The Holy Fox* |

## 4. The world they planned (the documents)

| Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Britain:** the *Sonderfahndungsliste G.B.* (the "Black Book"), 1940. It lists about 2,820 people to be arrested after an invasion, Churchill among them. It was an appendix to the *Informationsheft G.B.* handbook. Two copies are known: at the Imperial War Museum and the Hoover Institution. | H | IWM; en.wikipedia "The Black Book (list)" |
| **The East, food:** the minutes of the state secretaries' meeting of 2 May 1941 say "zig Millionen" (tens of millions) of people would "undoubtedly starve" if the army fed itself from Soviet territory. This was seven weeks before the invasion. | H | Kay, "Germany's Staatssekretäre, Mass Starvation and the Meeting of 2 May 1941", *J. Contemporary History* 41(4), 2006; Nuremberg document 2718-PS |
| **The East, land:** Generalplan Ost. The RSHA version reckoned about 45 million people in the target lands and called for about 31 million of them to be removed east to Siberia over 30 years, replaced by German settlers. The plan exists in several versions. | H (the plan) / M (the exact figures by version) | Wetzel memo, 27 Apr 1942; Cambridge, *Extermination of the European Jews*; Yad Vashem |
| **The Jews of Europe, including Britain:** the Wannsee Protocol (20 Jan 1942) lists Jews by country, with a total of "over 11 million". The list includes countries Germany never controlled: England 330,000 and Ireland 4,000. | H | The protocol (facsimile: GHDI); Jewish Virtual Library transcription |
| **Berlin:** Welthauptstadt Germania. Hitler meant it to be finished by 1950. The Great Hall would hold about 180,000 people. Building stopped in spring 1943. | H (the plan, the date, the stop) / M (180,000; repeated widely, check against Speer) | GHDI image 2335 (the Bundesarchiv model photo); en.wikipedia "Germania (city)" |
| **What still stands:** the *Schwerbelastungskörper* in Tempelhof. It is a concrete cylinder of about 12,650 tonnes, built in 1941 to test whether the marshy ground could carry the triumphal arch. It sank about 18 cm; the limit was 6 cm. It is a protected monument and can be visited. | H (built in 1941, the purpose, still there) / M (12,650 t; 18 cm) | Structurae; Berlin.de; en.wikipedia "Germania (city)" |

## 5. The stop line

**1950.** That's the year the regime set for its own capital. After it, the narrator says "from here, nobody knows."
