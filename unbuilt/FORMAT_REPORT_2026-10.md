# UNBUILT: what the winners do, and a new low-AI format (2 Oct 2026)

This was written at the owner's request after the 90-day test started.
- **Sources:** two research passes on 2 Oct 2026.
  - The YouTube Data API: 24 channels, their recent uploads, and searches for outlier videos.
  - The web: creator interviews, YouTube's own policy pages, audience-trust studies and revenue-per-view data.
- **YouTube figures** were read on 2 Oct 2026.
- **Confidence:** H means primary or measured, M means reputable secondary, L means weak.
- **Opinions are labelled.**

---

## 1. The short version

1. **Same topic, very different results.** Hoog's "The Evil Plan for Paris" (Le Corbusier's Plan Voisin) has 1.3M views; our Plan Voisin video has 128. **The topic isn't the problem. Execution and packaging are.** (H)
2. **Every channel that wins in this niche has one of two things** (H, measured):
   - a recognisable **human** voice or face: The B1M, Stewart Hicks, Dami Lee, Practical Engineering, Hiding In Plain Sight;
   - or very high-end **hand-made craft**: Mustard and Hoog use 3D, LEMMiNO uses motion graphics.
3. **Faceless AI channels that post a lot are all stuck around 1,000 views** (H, measured):

   | Channel | Long videos | Median long-video views |
   |---|---|---|
   | SkylineCollapse | 15 in 17 days | about 1.06K |
   | Unbuilt Giants | – | about 200 |
   | UNBUILT | – | most under 25 in the first days |

4. **The winners post rarely** (H):

   | Channel | How often it posts | Median views or subscribers |
   |---|---|---|
   | Mustard | about every 4 months | 5.9M median views |
   | Hoog | about monthly | 447K median |
   | Stewart Hicks | about monthly | 552K median |
   | Kings and Things | about every 2–3 months | 284K subscribers |

5. **Audiences trust AI presenters less** (M):
   - Only 19% of people are comfortable with artificial news presenters.
   - Human voices score 23% higher on trust.
   - Telling listeners a voice is AI cuts trust by 27%.

   **Whether that explains our 3-second exits is inference, not proven.**
6. **YouTube's money rules point the same way.** Since July 2025, "inauthentic", mass-produced content can't earn money. YouTube's CEO named managing "AI slop" as a 2026 priority. (H/M)

**My conclusion (opinion, well supported):**
- Your instinct is right, even though our own data can't prove AI is the cause.
- Rebuild UNBUILT as a **human-voiced, archive-led documentary channel**:
  - fewer, better episodes;
  - each one anchored to a city people know;
  - packaged the way the winners package theirs.
- No AI on screen.

## 2. What successful channels do that we don't

| What winners do | Evidence | What UNBUILT does now |
|---|---|---|
| **One consistent human voice or face**, so the channel *is* someone | B1M (Fred Mills), Stewart Hicks (a real architecture professor), Simon Whistler across 9+ channels, Hiding In Plain Sight ("I Found…") (H) | Three AI personas (Emmett, Hugo, Chiara) across two formats |
| **Hand-made hero visuals**: accurate 3D, real archive material, real places | Mustard: 2.39M subs from **60 videos**. Kings and Things' Boullée video, made from archival drawings: 1.97M. (H) | AI-generated reconstructions, which historians criticise for anachronisms (M) |
| **Few uploads, each heavily invested in** | Education channels posting 2–5 a month had twice the median subscribers of those posting 20–40 (M, correlational). YouTube warns that you "can exhaust your audience by uploading too much at once" (H). | 32 videos in 17 days, several within minutes of each other |
| **Short titles with one point of tension, anchored to a place** | "The Evil Plan for Paris" (1.3M); "Something Huge Was Left Unfinished in Sydney" (359K, on a 10K-sub channel); "Why New York City Can't Build" (H) | Long two-sentence titles, mostly anchored to an architect or a project rather than a place |
| **A number or a twist in the title** | "$8.8T Autopsy", "10 km Tall", "Built for 40,000 People… That Never Came" (H) | Sometimes |
| **A clear series identity** | Kings and Things' "Unbuilt Monuments of [City]" gets 250–306K per episode. Rene Ritchie (YouTube): use "an overarching framework that serves the same audience" (H/M). | Two formats, three hosts, plus countdown specials |
| **Every long video paired with a Short on the same day, as a trailer** | B1M and Dami Lee: those Shorts get 40K–1.6M views (H) | Many standalone Shorts with 1–421 views |
| **The payoff image within the first 5 seconds, the human angle first** | YouTube's 0:30 "Intro" metric is the measure. Dami Lee's "inverted pyramid" puts the human idea first (H/M). | AI-host cold opens |
| **Patience** | B1M took about 5 years to reach 1M after its pivot. Sponsorship usually starts at 50–100K subs. (H/M) | Judged after 17 days, which is a reason for the 90-day test |

**Small channels that broke out this year** show what's possible:
- **Hiding In Plain Sight** (started 2026): 22 videos and 10K subs. A first-person host covers local unfinished or abandoned projects ("Something Huge Was Left Unfinished in Sydney", 359K).
- **Tim Traveller**: a presenter visits real places. "The Belgian City That Built A Metro Line… And Never Opened It" has 2.45M.
- Both have a real person, a real place and a twist. **That combination is the model to copy.**

**Topics to avoid:** at least four small channels covered the Palace of the Soviets in recent weeks, at 1–2K views each. Unbuilt Giants and Kings and Things have also covered Newton's tomb, the Liverpool cathedral and Tour Sans Fins.

**A brand risk:** @unbuiltearthhq ("unbuilt") was created on 17 Sep 2026, one day after this channel, and posts Shorts under the same name.

## 3. The new format

**Working title: UNBUILT, "What should be standing here".**

**What each episode promises:**
- one never-built project;
- one city you know;
- the real documents;
- the real site today;
- a twist about what got built instead.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Length** | 10–14 minutes |
| **Cadence** | One episode every two weeks. That's within the test rule of at most one a week. |
| **Voice** | **One human voice:** yours (best), or one hired narrator. Hugo, Chiara and Emmett retire from new production. |
| **On camera** | Optional. Short pieces to camera at the real site when you can get there, in the Hiding In Plain Sight style. Otherwise voice-over. |
| **AI on screen** | **None.** No generated images, video or voices. AI is used only behind the scenes: research, fact-checking, script drafts that you edit, captions and the rights log. |
| **AI label** | Not needed for archive material and graphics. **Still needed** for any realistic composite of a building into a real place, even one built by hand in Blender. YouTube's rule covers realistic altered places whatever the tool. |
| **Shorts** | One trailer Short per episode, posted the same day, cut from the episode. No standalone Shorts. |

### Structure of an episode

| Time | Section | What's on screen | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | **The payoff** | The real site today (Google Earth or your own footage), with the original drawing overlaid where it would have stood | Shows what the title promised within seconds |
| 0:05–0:30 | **The question** | One human-stakes line, then the twist teased ("…and what they built instead is still there") | Protects retention at 0:30 |
| 0:30–3:00 | **The dream** | Original drawings and plans, period newspaper pages, a slow pan across the drawing with labels | Real documents are what we offer that others don't |
| 3:00–6:00 | **The scale** | Generated graphics: a scale comparison with a landmark viewers know, a map of the footprint over today's city | The "how big" moment |
| 6:00–9:00 | **Why it died** | Archive material, quotes on screen, a timeline | Story and tension |
| 9:00–11:30 | **What's there instead** | Present-day site: a Google Earth flyover, footage, then and now | The twist |
| 11:30–12:30 | **Sign-off** | One line, sources on screen, end screen to the next episode | Trust, and keeping people on the channel |

### Where the visuals come from (no generative AI)

1. **Public-domain archive material.** This is the core.
   - Most unbuilt projects survive as drawings. Anything published before 1931 is public domain in the US.
   - Sources: the Library of Congress (including Chronicling America newspapers), Wikimedia Commons, NYPL Digital Collections, Smithsonian Open Access, the Met and Getty open access, Gallica (BnF) and Europeana.
   - **Each asset's rights status goes in a rights log.**
2. **Google Earth Studio.** Free, with attribution, and used on YouTube by many large channels. It gives the "where it would have stood" flyovers.
3. **Code-generated graphics in one fixed house style.** I can build these with the Python and ffmpeg tools we already use, for no credits:
   - slow pans across scans;
   - drawings with arrows and labels;
   - newspaper highlights;
   - scale comparisons;
   - maps (Natural Earth, or OpenStreetMap with attribution);
   - timelines, lower thirds and end cards.
4. **Real footage.** What you shoot yourself; free stock (Pexels, Pixabay); public-domain film (NARA, Prelinger).
5. **Optional hero shot later: an accurate 3D model made by hand from the drawings**, using your existing Blender-to-Unreal pipeline (`unbuilt-pipeline`). This is the Mustard/Hoog layer: the highest ceiling, but it needs real 3D skill or a freelancer. Phase 3 only.
6. **Music:** **Correction, 3 Oct 2026:** this originally said "Epidemic Sound, already licensed". That was wrong. The connected Epidemic Sound account can't download tracks ("no permission"), and the earlier episodes used "The Architect's Parade", a track the owner generated elsewhere. Options for the new format:
   - a paid Epidemic Sound plan;
   - the YouTube Audio Library (free and cleared for YouTube);
   - public-domain recordings of public-domain music. **Content ID can wrongly claim these.**

   If the old track was made with an AI music tool, it doesn't fit the "made by people" promise.

**Rights rule:**
- Mid-century drawings are still in copyright: Frank Lloyd Wright (Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation / MoMA–Avery), Tange, and LIFE and TIME magazine pages.
- Use them only as short excerpts with commentary on screen, or license them.
- **Favour topics whose key images are public domain.**

### Packaging rules

1. **Titles:** 3–8 words, anchored to a place, with one point of tension. Add a number or a twist where it's true.
   - Good: "London Almost Built Its Own Eiffel Tower".
   - Avoid long titles that try to do two things.
2. **Thumbnails:** the real place today with the unbuilt structure as a drawn outline or a cut-out from the original drawing, plus at most 3 words.
   - **No AI images.**
   - Make 3 versions for YouTube's Test & Compare on every video.
3. **The first 5 seconds pay off the thumbnail.**
4. **Descriptions** list the sources, as the channel's trust signal.

### Choosing topics

Score each candidate from 0 to 2 on six things. Make it only if it scores 9 or more out of 12.

| Criterion | Score 0–2 |
|---|---|
| A **city or landmark** viewers know | |
| **Public-domain or licensable** key images | |
| A **twist**: what got built instead, or what is there now | |
| A real **number**: height, cost, people | |
| **Not covered** in the last 12 months by Unbuilt Giants, Kings and Things, Hoog or B1M | |
| A **site we can show today**: Google Earth, or one you can visit | |

## 4. Implementation plan

### Phase 0: decisions, this week (no cost)

1. **The voice.** Decide whether you'll narrate. This is the most important decision. If not, budget for a hired narrator (a narrator's cost per episode is not researched; expect roughly $100–300, **L**).
2. **The AI hosts.** Agree that Hugo, Chiara and Emmett retire from new production.
   - **Already finished videos still go out as scheduled.** Publishing them is free and the work is done: the Halloween special on 10 Oct, its Shorts, and the Wright special.
   - The 31 Oct Hugo decision becomes unnecessary.
3. **Pause the scripts in progress.** The myths and palaces specials stop in their current form and are re-planned in the new format. Their research is reused.
4. **The Higgsfield subscription.** If no AI production is planned, downgrade or don't renew at the next renewal. **That is the biggest saving: about $99–129 a month** (the Ultra price shown on 2 Oct).
5. **One production line.** Tell the other sessions to stop producing new AI episodes. The test rules in `90_DAY_TEST.md` already cap cadence.
6. **Basic gear if you narrate.** A decent USB microphone (a Shure MV7+ or Rode PodMic USB class, about $100–250, **L**) and a quiet, soft-furnished room.

### Phase 1: the toolkit, weeks 1–2 (no credits; my work, with your review)

1. **Archive fetcher.** It searches and downloads from the Library of Congress, Wikimedia Commons, NYPL, Smithsonian and Gallica, and records each item's licence and source URL in a rights log (`rights.csv`).
2. **Graphics library in the house style.** Slow pans, annotated drawings, newspaper highlights, scale comparisons, maps, timelines, lower thirds, end cards. One branded look across every episode.
3. **Assembly script.** It takes your recorded narration and a shot list, builds the cut, mixes music under the voice, and adds burned-in captions on the trailer Short. It's adapted from the existing `assemble.py` and `shorts.py`.
4. **Thumbnail compositor.** A real photo or Google Earth frame plus the original drawing plus text, in 3 versions.
5. **Episode template.** RESEARCH → SCRIPT, written for your voice → SHOTLIST, sourced from the archive → RIGHTS → PACKAGING.

## 5. Risks and what would prove this wrong

- **Your time and your voice are the real cost.** If you won't narrate, a hired narrator costs money every episode and gives you less of a recognisable identity.
- **Without AI renders there's less visual "wow".** Google Earth plus the real drawings plus clean graphics make up for most of it (Kings and Things gets 1–2M on archive art alone), and Phase 3's 3D adds it back.
- **Copyright** on 20th-century drawings and magazines. The topic scoring and the rights log manage this.
- **What would prove this wrong:** if Pilot A (the Watkin's Tower remake) does no better than its AI version on intro retention and % watched. Then AI wasn't the main problem, and topic choice and packaging matter more. Click-through rate would still be worth comparing.
- **This is a 1–3-year build, not a quick money-maker** (H/M: B1M's timeline; sponsorship typically starting at 50–100K subs). The 90-day test only tells you whether the new format is on a better path.
