# Asset inventory: Pilot A, Watkin's Tower

Compiled 2 Oct 2026. These are only real, existing visuals: no AI images and no reconstructions.

**Rights key:**
- **PD**: public domain. The basis is given in each row.
- **CC**: Creative Commons licence.
- **UNCLEAR**: rights unclear.
- **LICENCE**: licence or fee needed.

UK law: works by an unknown author are protected for 70 years from creation or publication, and works by a known author for 70 years after the author's death. A US "PD" tag (published before 1931) does **not** settle UK status.

**Before export:** download the highest resolution, keep the source URL in `rights_log`, and credit on screen or in the description even where it isn't required.

**Story beats used in the "Serves" column:** HOOK, WATKIN, COMP (competition), DESIGN, BUILD, FAIL, END, SITE (the stadium and today), SCALE.

## A. The 1890 competition catalogue (the core asset)

**A full public-domain scan exists.** It's at the Internet Archive, digitised in 2011 from the Getty Research Institute copy: https://archive.org/details/descriptiveillus00lynd
- 166 page images at 400 ppi, plus 2 foldouts.
- Files: PDF (5.6 MB), a JP2 zip (29.5 MB) and the **original JP2s as a tar (98.9 MB, best quality)**: https://archive.org/download/descriptiveillus00lynd/descriptiveillus00lynd_orig_jp2.tar
- Public Domain Review classes it as "PD Worldwide / No additional rights": https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/catalogue-of-the-68-competitive-designs-for-the-great-tower-for-london-1890/
- Rights basis: published 1890, with anonymous trade engravings ("Industries"). Copyright expired more than 70 years after publication. **PD, H.**
- Not checked on HathiTrust or Google Books. The Internet Archive copy is enough.
- Single page JPEG: `https://archive.org/download/descriptiveillus00lynd/page/n{leaf}_w2000.jpg`, where leaf ≈ printed page + 7. For example, n92 is p. 85.

## H. Google Earth Studio (shots of the site today)

Official sources:
- FAQ: https://www.google.com/earth/studio/faq/
- Attribution docs: https://earth.google.com/studio/docs/attribution/
- Google Geo Guidelines: https://about.google/brand-resource-center/products-and-services/geo-guidelines/

**Allowed (H):** Geo Guidelines, "Television, film, and online video": "If you're using Google Earth content in an online video (e.g. YouTube) primarily for educational, instructional, recreational, or entertainment purposes, you don't need to request permission, **even if your work is monetized**."

**Not allowed (H):** "You may not use Google Earth or Earth Studio content … for promotional films, advertisements or commercials."

**Attribution (H):** "Google Earth" plus any third-party imagery providers must be shown on screen **for the whole time** the content is visible. Earth Studio burns the attribution into each rendered frame automatically, and its position can be changed in Render Settings. The data providers listed change with the view. Don't remove, obscure or crop it; it must be legible and close to the content.

**Tension to note (M):** the Earth Studio FAQ also says "We currently do not offer a license to use Google Earth imagery for commercial applications." The Geo Guidelines carve out monetised educational and entertainment YouTube video, so I read that as permitted. That is my reading, not legal advice.

**Thumbnail risk (opinion, L–M):** a thumbnail is promotional packaging. Using a Google Earth frame there without the attribution, or arguably at all, is a grey area. If one is used, keep the attribution visible.

**Planned shots (opinion):**
1. A slow push from Wembley Park station along Olympic Way to the stadium (the Watkin railway → park → tower line).
2. A top-down shot of the pitch, matching the 1922 aerial (#24 and #25) for a dissolve.
3. An orbit at 366 m, showing how high the design would have reached, with a code-made outline added in the edit, not AI.
4. Baker Street → Wembley along the Metropolitan line, to illustrate the "12 minutes".

## Gaps and blockers

- **No blocker on the catalogue:** a full PD scan exists at 400 ppi.
- **Photos of the stump are small on Commons** (about 500 px wide). For full-screen use, the Brent originals (£125–£250 each) or careful slow pans are needed. Upscaling with AI would break the no-AI rule, so don't.
- **The best evidence for the site** (the 1922 craters inside the stadium) is Brent #39 or Aerofilms #25. Both need licences. The free fallback is #24 (Commons, 1374 px).
- Not found: a Watkin photograph in the Library of Congress; any *Punch* or *ILN* item; early Metropolitan Railway posters for Wembley Park in PD form (the only one found is Brent #34).
