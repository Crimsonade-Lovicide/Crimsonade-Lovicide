# Packaging: Pilot A, Watkin's Tower

**Everything in this file is OPINION or a BET.** It isn't evidence.

Each line rests on facts labelled in `RESEARCH.md`, and every title claim is checked against them. The rival titles referred to are in `COMPETITION.md`.

The rules from the brief: 3–8 words, anchored to a place, one point of tension, and a number or twist only where it's true.

**Rejected:** "…Could Have Been a Vegetarian Colony" (not supported by the source); "taller than the Shard" (true of the design, but anchored to the wrong landmark); anything saying "under the centre circle" (L).

### Top pick A: #1, "London Almost Built Its Own Eiffel Tower"

**Case for:** It's the owner's working title. "London's Eiffel Tower" is the phrase every rival uses, so it's a proven search phrase. It's also close to our best Short ("London's Eiffel tower", 1,643 views, per `FORMAT_REPORT_2026-10.md`).

**Strongest case against:** the space is crowded.
- Focus Point already has "How London Almost Had An Eiffel Tower" (25.6K views).
- Unbuilt Giants' "London's Eiffel Tower That Was Never Finished" got **95 views** on 11.4K subscribers.

The same frame can fail completely. "Almost built" also undersells the fact that part of it really was built and people went up it.

**What would prove it wrong:** a click-through rate in the first 48 hours no better than our AI version's.

### Top pick B: #2, "What They Found Under Wembley's Pitch"

**Case for:** Wembley is a world-famous place anchor. The title creates a curiosity gap that no rival long-form video uses, and it leads straight into the archive-led site match (1922 aerial view → today).

**Strongest case against:**
- It's the most clickbait-like option, and the payoff ("concrete foundations") is modest.
- The claim is **M**, resting on one reported source.
- The Build Explained used almost the same line for a Short and got about 1K views.
- Football fans may click expecting football and leave, which hurts retention, the very metric the pilot is meant to test.

**What would prove it wrong:** high click-through but poor 30-second retention.

**A note on the test (opinion):** the AI version's title was "London Started Its Own Eiffel Tower. It Got 154 Feet Up." Using a similar frame (#1 or #8) keeps the comparison closer to "format only". A new frame (#2) adds a second variable. Choose deliberately.

## Thumbnail concepts (real images only)

These use only real images, a Google Earth frame and code-drawn lines or text. No AI, no upscaling and no AI "restoration".

### T1: "1,200 planned, 155 built" (recommended to pair with title #1)

**Image:**
- The Design No. 37 plate (ASSETS #3, PD) full height on the left.
- On the right, the real first-stage photo (ASSETS #14 or #19, PD) scaled to the true ratio: 155 ft against 1,200 ft, about 13%.
- A code-drawn bracket at the base, in the brand accent colour.

**Text:** "1,200 FT" in thin type at the top, "155 BUILT" in bold at the bottom.

**Case against:**
- Engraved plates are thin black lines on cream, which reads weakly on mobile. Contrast and levels adjustments are fine; redrawing is not.
- The real stump at true scale is tiny, so the tension may not read at 168 px wide.
- It's also close to the logic of our old title, so it may look like the same video.

### T2: "Then and now" (pairs with title #2 or #6)

**Image:** a split frame.
- Left: the 1922 Aerofilms craters (ASSETS #24, PD on Commons). It would be stronger with the licensed Brent #39 or Historic England EPW007831.
- Right: a Google Earth Studio top-down shot of today's stadium, framed the same way.
- A single code-drawn circle on the crater in both halves.

**Text:** "UNDER THE PITCH".

**Case against:**
- Aerial photos are abstract at thumbnail size.
- The Commons scan is only 1374 px.
- **Google Earth content in a thumbnail is a policy grey area.** It's promotional packaging, and the Geo Guidelines bar promotional use and require visible attribution. Keep "Google Earth" legible, or swap the right half for the 1924 INS aerial (ASSETS #29, PD-US, 3521 px).
- The overlay precision is L, so the circle must not suggest an exact spot.

### T3: "The brief" (pairs with title #4)

**Image:** a tight crop of the 1889 specification (ASSETS #2, PD), with "assume that the foundations are perfect" picked out by a code-drawn underline, set over a dark crop of the stump photo.

**Text:** none, or "PERFECT?".

**Case against:**
- Text-on-text, with low emotional pull. A document thumbnail rarely beats an image of the thing.
- It only works if the viewer reads small serif type on a phone, which most won't.

## The 5-second opening (pays off T1 and title #1)

| Time | Picture | Narration (owner's voice) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Design No. 37 plate full frame, slow tilt from base to tip | "In 1890, London chose a tower taller than the Eiffel Tower." |
| 0:03–0:05 | Hard cut to the real first-stage photo; a code-drawn line marks 155 ft | "This is as far as it got." |

**Fact check:**
- Chosen in 1890: the prize was awarded in 1890 (H).
- Taller: 1,200 ft against the Eiffel Tower's 984–1,024 ft in 1889 (H).
- The photo shows the first stage, about 155 ft (H).

**If T2 and title #2 are chosen instead:**

| Time | Picture | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Google Earth top-down of Wembley with attribution visible; slow push in | "In 2002, builders lowering Wembley's pitch hit old concrete." |
| 0:03–0:05 | Dissolve to the 1922 craters | "It belonged to a tower meant to beat the Eiffel Tower." |

The first line is M: say "reportedly" in the second sentence, or put the source on screen.

**Case against both openings:** five seconds of "what it was" before "why it matters" can feel like a Wikipedia lede. The clause-1 irony (title #4) is the stronger hook intellectually, but it needs about 10 seconds to land. That trade-off is the owner's call, and a reason to test the T1 versus T3 thumbnails if A/B testing is available on the channel.
