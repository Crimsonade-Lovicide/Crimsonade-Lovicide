In 1890, London held a competition to beat the Eiffel Tower. Sixty-eight designs came in, and the winner was 1,200 feet tall. One stage was built. And the problem was there in clause one of the brief: "The designer will assume that the foundations are perfect."

This is the story of Watkin's Tower: Sir Edward Watkin's plan to fill his railway with day-trippers, the wildest of the 68 entries (a captive parachute, a 2,000-foot Egyptian colony in the sky), and why the tower that did go up sank, ran out of money and was pulled down, its foundations blown up with dynamite in 1907. Then builders came back to the same hilltop, and today it's one of the most famous football pitches in the world.
