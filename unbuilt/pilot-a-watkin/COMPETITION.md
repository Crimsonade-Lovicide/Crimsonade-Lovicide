## What they miss that we could own (OPINION, not verified by watching)

1. **The brief's own words: "assume that the foundations are perfect"** (clause 1, 1 Nov 1889). No title, description or chapter shows it. It gives the story a spine built on a primary source, not a list of wild designs. *Case against:* Robslondon spends 6.5 minutes on the catalogue and may well read the spec aloud.
2. **A proper site match:** the 1922 aerial view of the four craters inside the rising stadium, dissolving to a Google Earth top-down shot today, with the confidence stated on screen ("the craters sat inside the bowl; foundations found under the pitch in 2002"). Rivals say "on the site" or "under the pitch" without showing it. *Case against:* the best version of that image needs a licence (Brent or Historic England). The free Commons copy is 1374 px wide.
3. **Corrections as content:** the "vegetarian colony" myth, "12,000 visitors", "under the Twin Towers", the 1,175 ft versus 1,200 ft height. A "what the internet gets wrong" segment suits a human-narrated, archive-led format. *Case against:* debunking can read as petty, and it means admitting our own Short was wrong. That's honest, but it's a choice for the owner.
4. **The Hills → West Ham → 1923 Cup Final loop** as a closing beat (facts H; whether it's a good closer is opinion). Not seen in any metadata. *Case against:* football and architecture audiences overlap less than you'd think, and it's a coincidence, not a cause.
5. **Blackpool Tower** (158 m), opened in the same month the Wembley pleasure grounds opened, as the "what realistic looked like" contrast. *Case against:* it adds a second location and may dilute the Wembley anchor.
6. ***The Engineer*, 8 Sep 1893, construction drawings.** No rival credits them. *Case against:* I haven't seen the plates, so they may be only text or small diagrams.

**What would prove this wrong:** if watching Robslondon, Zepherus and Jago shows they already cover points 1, 2 and 4, then our edge is only format (the human voice and archive honesty), not content. Then the title and thumbnail have to carry more of the weight.

## Implications for the pilot test (OPINION)

The pilot compares the remake with our AI version (163 views). The same topic now has a fresh rival: Tom Goes Nomad posted two weeks ago and is at 10K. That raises the bar for search traffic and shows recent demand exists. If the remake beats 163 views but stays far below 10K, that tells us little about format alone.
