# Research and fact-check: Pilot A, Watkin's Tower (Wembley Park, 1889–1907)

Researched 2 Oct 2026 for the low-AI remake. Confidence: **H** = primary source, or two solid sources agree. **M** = one good secondary source, or sources that differ slightly. **L** = weak, single blog or disputed. Script wording should hedge every M and avoid every L.

**Main primary source:** Lynde, F. C. (ed.), *Descriptive Illustrated Catalogue of the Sixty-Eight Competitive Designs for the Great Tower for London* (London: "Industries", 1890). A full scan exists on the Internet Archive (Getty Research Institute copy, 400 ppi): https://archive.org/details/descriptiveillus00lynd. I read the OCR text and checked key pages against the page images. It's cited below as **[Cat.]**, with the printed page.

## 9. Myths and common errors to avoid

1. **"A vegetarian colony" or "the London Vegetarian Society's design."** The catalogue entry (No. 38, p. 85) says "aerial colony" and "Flower and Fruit Shows". It **never mentions vegetarians, hanging vegetable gardens or growing food.** The entrant was A. F. Hills of Thames Iron Works, who happened to be president of the London Vegetarian Society. The "aerial vegetarians" line goes back to a 2006 Guardian piece via [WP]. **This affects our own Short** ("…Could Have Been a Vegetarian Colony. True Story.", FHJz4RUhKaY). It overstates the source, and the owner may want to correct or retire it. (H that the catalogue doesn't say it.)
2. **"A £1m tower inspired by the Leaning Tower of Pisa"** ([WP]). The £1,000,000 entry in the catalogue is No. 18, the parachute tower. I found no Pisa reference in the OCR text. L, so avoid it unless a plate clearly shows one.
3. **"12,000 visitors in 1895"** ([WP]). Brent's local historian gives 120,000. Probably a dropped zero.
4. **"Liquidation in 1889"** (appears in a Public Domain Review-derived summary). Impossible, since the company was formed in 1889. It was 1898 or 1899.
5. **"358 m / 1,175 ft"** given as the design height. The primary figure is 1,200 ft.
6. **"The winning design was built."** The octagon was cut to four legs, so what stood was a cheaper redesign.
7. **"Demolished in 1907"** (on its own). Demolition ran from 1904; the foundations were dynamited in Sept 1907.
8. **"Foundations under the Twin Towers."** They were under the pitch (section 7).
9. **"London Stump"** as a period nickname. Unverified.
10. **Remake History's "1982" in a Short title.** Simply wrong.
11. Spelling: the plate says **MacLaren**, while the preface and many retellings say McLaren. Use "MacLaren", as on the design plate.
12. **"Antiquitech/Tartaria" retellings** (Jarid Boosters, 38K views) treat these towers as a lost civilisation. It's conspiracy content. Don't engage with it, and don't use its footage.
13. Eiffel heights: the catalogue's 300 m (structure) and Wikipedia's 312 m (with flag) are both "1889 heights". Say which one you mean.

## 10. The twist or spine (OPINION, not fact)

These are suggestions for the owner to judge. The facts they rest on are labelled above.

- **Spine (opinion):** *The brief told 68 designers to "assume that the foundations are perfect". The tower failed at its foundations, and the place where it failed became the most famous patch of grass in England.* It runs from a primary-source quote (H) through subsidence (H) to Wembley (H), and it's a frame no competitor title or chapter list shows. I can't see whether they mention clause 1 inside their videos.
- **Twist candidate (opinion, facts H):** the man behind the wildest Egyptian-pyramid entry, Arnold Hills, co-founded the football club that became **West Ham United**. West Ham played in the **first FA Cup Final at the new stadium in 1923**, on the ground built over the tower's craters. *Case against:* it's a coincidence, not a cause, and football fans may see it as a stretch. Use it as a closing beat, not the hook. (Hills had stopped backing the club by about 1900; check the 1923 Bolton v West Ham line-up and result before scripting.)
- **Contrast beat (opinion, facts H):** Blackpool Tower, 158 m, opened 14 May 1894, the same month Wembley Park opened. A smaller, realistic copy of Eiffel succeeded while the over-tall one failed.
- **What not to claim (opinion):** that Watkin's stroke "killed" the tower. Money, ground and weak demand all came first or alongside it, and the order of causes isn't settled in the sources I found.
