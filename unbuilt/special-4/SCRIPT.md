# UNBUILT Special No. 4: "Palaces Kings Never Got" (working title)

**Status:** first draft for review, 1 Oct 2026. No shots planned or rendered, and no credits spent.
**Runtime:** about 7:45. **Format:** 16:9, 720p (1080p upload master). **Host:** Hugo Ashby, on camera and in VO, voice Arthur. It is written so it also works as voice-over only, if Hugo is dropped on 31 Oct; see the notes.
**Release target (proposed):** Sat 5 Dec 2026, a winter special.

**YouTube title (proposed):** *Catherine the Great Tore Down the Kremlin Wall for a Palace She Never Built*

Alternatives:
- *Napoleon, Catherine the Great and the Mad King: 5 Palaces That Were Never Built*
- *5 Royal Palaces That Were Never Built*

**The spine: how close each one came.** It climbs from drawings, to a road up the mountain, to a cornerstone laid by the king, to 105 houses bought and a hill dug away, to a Kremlin wall actually torn down. The scale climbs with it, to more than 600 metres.

**Built on the week-one readout:**
1. **The cold open pays off inside 10 seconds.** Its first line is the Kremlin wall coming down. The title sting is 1.5 s.
2. **No. 5 opens on its best image:** a palace beside the Parthenon.
3. **Proper names everywhere:** Catherine, Napoleon, Ludwig, Bernini, the Louvre, the Acropolis.

**Tone.** Kings and their vanity are fair game. People are not. Ludwig's death is told plainly, with no joke. Crimea gets one neutral mention and Russia appears only as history.

**Legend**
- `SYNC`: Hugo on camera, lip-synced.
- `VO`: Hugo over b-roll.
- **Pace:** about 2.6 words per second.
- **Confidence tags:** `(M)` marks a figure that is single-sourced or approximate; the wording already hedges it. Everything else is H in `RESEARCH_palaces.md`.

## No. 5 · A PALACE ON THE ACROPOLIS · Athens, 1834 (0:35–1:40)

**[A1] SYNC** · the Acropolis in morning light, Hugo on the plateau with the Parthenon behind him
> This is the Acropolis. In 1834, Prussia's greatest architect proposed building a royal palace right here, next door.

**[A2] VO** · 1830s Athens from the hill: a small town of low houses below the ancient rock
> Greece had a new king: Otto, a teenager from Bavaria. Karl Friedrich Schinkel drew him a palace on the empty end of the hill. He had never been to Athens.

**[A3] VO** · Schinkel's palace: low colonnaded wings and courtyards on the plateau, the Parthenon standing in a landscaped garden beside it

**[A4] VO** · a Munich study; a rival architect reading the drawings by lamplight

**[A5] VO** · Crimea: a cliff over the Black Sea, a glass temple on a vast plinth above a terrace of caryatids

**[A6] SYNC** · the Acropolis, Hugo glancing back at the Parthenon

## No. 4 · FALKENSTEIN · Bavaria, 1883 (1:40–2:45)

**[F1] SYNC** · a snowy Alpine summit, the low broken walls of a medieval ruin, Hugo in the wind
> This is Falkenstein, in the Bavarian Alps. The king who built Neuschwanstein decided it wasn't enough.

**[F2] VO** · Ludwig II's castle as designed: a soaring Gothic fortress of towers and spires on the summit, clouds below
> In 1883, Ludwig the Second bought this ruin and ordered a new castle on top. The first sketch came from a painter of theatre sets.

**[F3] VO** · an architect at a drafting table, a royal letter in a sealed envelope beside him; the drawings tossed aside
> One architect drew a sensible version. The king rejected it, furiously. The next one resigned.

**[F4] VO** · the planned bedroom: a Byzantine chapel with a blue dome of gold stars, walls of gold mosaic, the bed set in an apse like an altar
> And the bedroom kept growing. It became a Byzantine church: a dome of gold stars, walls of mosaic, and the bed in an apse, like an altar.

**[F5] VO** · a mountain road being cut through snowy pines; then a grey lake at dusk
> They built a road up the mountain, and a water pipe. In June 1886, Ludwig was declared insane and stripped of power. Days later, he was found dead in Lake Starnberg. His architect was drawing the fourth version of the bedroom.

**[F6] SYNC** · the summit, Hugo looking down the valley towards a distant white castle

## No. 3 · BERNINI'S LOUVRE · Paris, 1665 (2:45–4:00)

**[B1] SYNC** · the east front of the Louvre: the long colonnade of paired columns, Hugo on the pavement before it
> This is the east front of the Louvre. It is not the one the king ordered.

**[B2] VO** · June 1665, Saint-Germain-en-Laye: a white-haired Italian bowing to the young Louis XIV, the court in silks around them, seen from behind
> In 1665, Louis the Fourteenth summoned the most famous artist in Europe, Gian Lorenzo Bernini, from Rome. At his first audience, Bernini turned to the courtiers and said: "Let no one speak to me of anything small."

**[B3] VO** · Bernini's final design: a vast palace front rising from a base carved as raw rock, giant columns above, statues along the roofline
> He gave the king a palace front rising from a base carved to look like a mountain of raw rock. Louis loved it, and called Bernini "the ablest man in Europe."

**[B4] VO** · Paris from the hills: a sea of chimneys and steep slate roofs
> Bernini was less taken with Paris. The skyline, he said, was "a heap of chimneys." The Tuileries palace was "a big little thing." A French critic, he told Charles Perrault, wasn't fit to clean the soles of his shoes.

**[B5] VO** · 17 October 1665: the king kneels at a foundation trench with a silver trowel, a gold medal set into the stone
> On the seventeenth of October, the king laid the cornerstone. Bernini handed him the trowel. Three days later, Bernini went home to Rome.

**[B6] VO** · Colbert at his desk by candlelight, writing; then the colonnade rising, 1670
> The king's minister, Colbert, worried about the noise, the doors, the rain on the flat roof, and the cost. In 1667, the design was dropped, and a French council designed the colonnade instead. The leading name among them: Perrault.

**[B7] VO** · Versailles, a far corner by a lake: a marble horseman in a Roman helmet, flames at the horse's feet
> Bernini also carved the king on horseback. Louis hated it, and nearly had it smashed. Instead it was recut into a Roman hero and banished to the far end of a lake at Versailles.

**[B8] SYNC** · the Cour Napoléon, Hugo beside a lead equestrian statue near the glass pyramid
> In 1985, the architect of the Louvre pyramid chose a copy of that statue to stand here. So Bernini got into the Louvre in the end. On horseback.

## No. 2 · THE PALACE OF THE KING OF ROME · Paris, 1811 (4:00–5:15)

**[N1] SYNC** · the Trocadéro terrace, the Eiffel Tower directly behind Hugo across the river
> This is the Trocadéro, the best view of the Eiffel Tower in Paris. Napoleon wanted it for a palace. For a baby.

**[N2] VO** · Paris, 1811: cannon fired from the Invalides, crowds cheering a royal birth
> In February 1811, before his son was even born, Napoleon decreed a palace for him: the King of Rome.

**[N3] VO** · the palace on the Chaillot hill: a 400-metre front of colonnades, terraces dropping to the Seine, the Bois de Boulogne beyond
> Its front would be four hundred metres long, as long as Versailles. There would be stables for four hundred horses, a hall to receive the kings of Europe, and the Bois de Boulogne for a garden.

**[N4] VO** · labourers levelling the hill; a single small house left standing on the line of the façade, its owner in the doorway
> A hundred and five houses were bought, and the hill was dug away. One landowner held out until they paid him a hundred and seventy thousand francs. In exile, Napoleon told it differently: a cooper whose price kept rising, and a house left standing "as a monument of my respect for the laws." (M)

**[N5] VO** · retreat from Moscow in snow; then workmen filling in the foundations
> Then came Russia. Then came Waterloo. The new king filled in the foundations. Napoleon claimed he'd "never thought of doing more than preparing the ground."

**[N6] VO** · the Palais de Chaillot, 1948: delegates in a great hall
> His son never lived in Paris. He died in Austria at twenty-one. And in 1948, on this hill, the United Nations adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

**[N7] SYNC** · the Trocadéro at dusk, the tower lighting up
> A palace for a boy who was born a king. Instead, the place where the world wrote down that everyone is born free and equal.

- **Hedged on purpose, marked (M):**
  - Klenze's line (single source).
  - The Empress's line (single source).
  - The cooper story, attributed to Napoleon's own telling. Poisson's documentary Nettement figure (170,000 F) is given as the fact.
  - Bazhenov "trained in Paris": he studied at the Paris Academy, but check the details.
- **Myths avoided:**
  - "Bigger than Versailles": it is "as long as" (400 m against 402 m).
  - Napoleon's "Kremlin a hundred times more beautiful" (weak source).
  - "20,000 rooms."
  - Thon's 1849 Grand Kremlin Palace: never shown.
  - Ludwig "deposed": he was declared insane and stripped of power under a regency.
  - Construction at Falkenstein: only the road and pipe were built.
  - "Rien qui soit petit" said to the king: it was said to the courtiers.
  - The Louvre statue is not "Bernini's Louis XIV". It's a lead cast of the recut Roman-hero version, chosen by I. M. Pei in 1985.
  - The colonnade is credited to "a French council… the leading name: Perrault", not to Perrault alone.
  - "The Parthenon in the courtyard": it would have been in the garden, beside the palace.
  - The Bazhenov model is "in a Moscow museum". It has been under restoration since 2022, so don't say "on display".
