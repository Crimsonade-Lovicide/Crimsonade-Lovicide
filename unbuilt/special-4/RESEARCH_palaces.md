# Research: palaces that were never built (Special No. 4 candidate)

Fact-check of 30 Sep 2026. Confidence: **H** means two or more solid sources agree, or the primary source was read. **M** means one good source, or sources disagree slightly. **L** means weak or disputed; L claims are not used as fact.

## 1. Bazhenov's Grand Kremlin Palace, for Catherine the Great (1767–75). Proposed No. 1

| Item | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Dates | First drafts 1767, design final 1768. Foundation ceremony **1 June 1773**. Halted 1775. | H: en/ru Wikipedia; MUAR https://muar.ru/exhibitions/model-bolshogo-kremlevskogo-dvortsa-arkhitektora-vasiliya-bazhenova/ |
| Design | Four storeys wrapping the south side of the Kremlin hill, from the Konstantino-Yeleninskaya Tower to the Borovitskaya, then north along the west wall, with an oval forum inside. It would have been Europe's largest neoclassical complex. | H |
| Length | About 600 m (ru.wiki), 630 m / 2,100 ft (en.wiki) or 639 m (Lenta, 2025). **Say "more than 600 metres, about half as long again as Versailles' 402 m façade."** | M |
| Model | Limewood, with maple cornices and pear and apple wood interiors. **Over 17 m long**, 1.3 m high and about 6 m wide; the centre comes apart to show the state rooms. On public view from May 1771. The 40,000-rouble cost is L. The scale is 1:44 or about 1:48 (M). | H on size and materials |
| The wall | Catherine's order of March 1771: "сломать городовую стену по Москве-реке…" ("break down the city wall along the Moscow River"). The Taynitskaya, Petrovskaya and First Unnamed Towers were dismantled, with the wall between them. The wall was rebuilt by 1783 under Kazakov. (ru.wiki gives the Taynitskaya as rebuilt in 1776, which conflicts.) | H: https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Тайницкая_башня |
| Ceremony | 1 June 1773, at the Taynitskaya gate site, with a temporary staircase, a "Temple of Russian Glory" and an inscribed copper plaque. Catherine did not attend. | H event, M her absence: https://mr.moscow/vasilij-bazhenov-kremlyovskij-proekt/ |
| Quote | "**Въ сей день обновляется Москва**": "On this day Moscow is renewed." From *Слово на заложеніе Кремлевскаго дворца* ("Speech at the laying of the Kremlin palace"), printed in Sumarokov's works (1787): https://ru.wikisource.org/?curid=1005262. en.wiki attributes it to Bazhenov, so **say "the speech at the ceremony"**. | H text, M speaker |
| Inscription | "For the glory of the great empire, for the honour of its age, for the immortal memory of future times, for the adornment of the capital city…" | M |
| Why it died | The official reason was subsidence and cracks at the Archangel Cathedral, on unstable ground. Historians add: the cost (estimates of 30–60 million roubles), the Turkish war, the Pugachev rebellion, Catherine's dislike of Moscow, and possibly Bazhenov's Freemasonry. | H official, M others |
| Site now | The rebuilt south wall and towers. No Bazhenov building. | H |
| Model now | Shchusev Museum of Architecture, Talyzin House. It went on display in 2012 and **closed for restoration on 19 Sep 2022. Check its status.** | H |
| Twist | The wall really was torn down, then put back. A decade later Catherine had Bazhenov's nearly finished Tsaritsyno palace demolished (1785–86) after one visit, calling it dark and "unfit for living." | H: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Bazhenov |
| Halt | Spring 1775, during Catherine's visit to Moscow: the cathedrals' buttresses settled and cracked, and the trench was filled. No decree text was found, so don't quote one. | H (reason) |
| Myths | Today's "Grand Kremlin Palace" is Thon's, built 1838–49; don't use its image. The "20,000 rooms" figure is L. "Life-size model" is a mistranslation. The 120-sledge trip to St Petersburg is dated 1772 or 1773 (M). | |

## 2. Napoleon's Palace of the King of Rome, Chaillot hill (Percier & Fontaine, 1811–13). Proposed No. 2

| Item | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Dates | Decided February 1811, before the son was born on 20 March 1811. Decree of 16 Feb 1811, with 20 million francs. Scaled back March 1813. Only earthworks existed by late 1813. | H: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_du_roi_de_Rome ; https://shannonselin.com/2016/04/palace-of-the-king-of-rome/ |
| Design | **400 m façade**, a semi-elliptical colonnade, and a festival hall "to receive the kings of Europe." Stables for 400 horses and 80 carriages. The Bois de Boulogne as its park, and an "imperial city" around the Champ-de-Mars. | H (fr.wiki, citing Wahl 1955) |
| Against Versailles | The architects wrote that it "eussent été déjà un grand avantage sur… Versailles" ("would already have had a great advantage over… Versailles") (*Revue de Paris*, 1833). Its 400 m against Versailles' 402 m means "rival" is fair, **"bigger" is not**. | M |
| Fontaine | "l'ouvrage le plus vaste et le plus extraordinaire de notre siècle" ("the vastest and most extraordinary work of our century") | M |
| How far | 503 plots, 105 houses and a mill were bought for 1,724,000 francs. Convent ruins were cleared and the earthworks faced the Pont d'Iéna. The Bourbons razed the foundations. | H |
| Quote 1 | "…nothing is so expensive or so truly useless as this multitude of palaces: and if, nevertheless, I undertook that of the King of Rome, it was because I had views peculiar to myself; and besides, in reality, I never thought of doing more than preparing the ground. There I should have stopped." Las Cases, *Memorial*, vol. III: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/53969 | H (primary) |
| Landowner | Poisson, *Napoléon et Paris* (2002), p. 215, via fr.wiki: M. Nettement asked 500,000 F and settled for 170,000 F. This is better documented than the cooper story. | M |
| Quote 2 | A cooper, "Bonvivant", pushed his price from 10,000 to 50,000 francs. Napoleon: "Cet homme là est un misérable… je n'achèterai point la maison, et elle restera comme un monument de mon respect pour les lois" ("That man is a wretch… I will not buy the house, and it shall stand as a monument to my respect for the law"). O'Meara, *Napoleon in Exile* (1822), vol. II: https://archive.org/details/b29340470_0002 | H that it is printed; M that it happened (it's an anecdote). fr.wiki has a different owner, Nettement. |
| Why it died | The defeats of 1812–13, then the fall of the Empire. | H |
| Site now | Place du Trocadéro (1867), Palais du Trocadéro (1878), **Palais de Chaillot** (1937). The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** was adopted there on 10 Dec 1948; Eleanor Roosevelt chaired the drafting committee. | H |
| Twist | The classic Eiffel Tower view is taken from this spot. A dynastic palace site became where human rights were proclaimed. The son never lived in Paris and died at 21, in Austria. | H |
| Avoid | "Un kremlin cent fois plus beau que celui de Moscou" ("a Kremlin a hundred times more beautiful than Moscow's") comes only from a 2002 *Historia* article (L). | |
| Drawings | Percier & Fontaine, *Résidences de souverains* (1833); plates on Commons. | M |

## 3. Bernini's Louvre, for Louis XIV (1664–67). Proposed No. 3

The second fact-check, 1 Oct 2026, read two primary sources: Chantelou's journal, in the Lalanne edition of 1885 (https://archive.org/download/journalduvoyage00lalagoog/journalduvoyage00lalagoog_djvu.txt), and Colbert's letters, Clément edition vol. 5.

| Item | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Designs | **First design, June 1664:** a convex oval pavilion between two concave arms. **Second:** a concave centre. **Third, Paris, June 1665:** a straight block on a base carved as artificial rock ("un écueil ou espèce de rocher", a reef or kind of rock), with a giant Corinthian order above. **Say "three main designs, plus revisions."** Sources count 3, 4 or 5. | H / M (count) |
| Width | East front over 150 m. The colonnade actually built is 183 m. No reliable height, so give none. | M |
| Encasing | The final design wrapped the old Cour Carrée in a new skin. Bernini, 2 Aug 1665: "Sa Majesté ayant voulu conserver le Louvre l'avait détruit" ("by wishing to keep the Louvre, His Majesty had destroyed it"). | M–H |
| Visit | Met at Juvisy on 2 June 1665. Left Paris 20 Oct 1665. | H |
| "Nothing small" | **4 June 1665**, at Saint-Germain-en-Laye. To Louis he said: "il faut faire pour un roi de France… de plus grandes et magnifiques choses" ("for a king of France one must make greater and more magnificent things"). Then, **turning to the courtiers**: **"Qu'on ne me parle de rien qui soit petit"**, "Let no one speak to me of anything small." The French wording is Chantelou's; Bernini spoke Italian. | H |
| The rock base | 20 June 1665: Louis chose the rock base and called Bernini "le plus habile homme de l'Europe" ("the ablest man in Europe"). | H |
| Barbs | 13 June: the Val-de-Grâce dome is "une bien petite calotte sur une grosse tête" ("a very small skullcap on a big head"). 23 July: the Tuileries is "una grande picciola cosa" ("a big little thing"). 2 Aug: Paris is "un amas de cheminées… comme un peigne à carder" ("a heap of chimneys… like a carding comb"). 6 Oct, to Charles Perrault: not worthy "de nettoyer la semelle de ses souliers" ("to clean the soles of his shoes"). | H |
| Cornerstone | **17 Oct 1665.** Louis XIV laid it, Bernini handed him the silver trowel, and Jean Warin's medal of the façade went into the stone. The medal's reverse reads "MAIESTATI. AC. AETERNIT. GALL. IMPERII. SACRVM." ("Sacred to the majesty and eternity of the French empire"). | H |
| Colbert | Practical objections: comfort, security (too many doors), climate (open arcades, flat roof), and the king's rooms on the noisy street front. Not taste. 28 Sep 1665: "Ô quelle pitié, que le plus grand roy… fust mesuré à l'aune de Versailles!" ("Oh, what a pity that the greatest king… should be measured by the yardstick of Versailles!") | M–H / H |
| End | The Petit Conseil (Le Vau, Le Brun, Claude Perrault), April 1667. Louis chose the colonnade on 14 May 1667. Bernini was told around July 1667. The colonnade was built 1667–c.1674. Its attribution is disputed, so say "a French council, the leading name Perrault." | M–H |
| Statue | Commissioned 1667 and carved in Rome. At Versailles by 1685. Dangeau, 14 Nov 1685: the king found "l'homme et le cheval… si mal faits" ("the man and the horse… so badly made") that he resolved to remove it, "même de la faire briser" ("even to have it broken up"). Girardon recut it as **Marcus Curtius** in 1687. Sent to the far end of the Pièce d'eau des Suisses in 1702. The original is now in the Orangerie, with a copy outside. | H |
| Louvre copy | **I. M. Pei chose it in 1985.** A lead cast (Coubertin, 1986) of the Marcus Curtius version stands in the Cour Napoléon by the pyramid. | H / M (year of the cast) |
| Avoid | "Said to the king." "Colbert thought it ugly." "Perrault alone." "Bernini's Louis XIV at the Louvre." "Destroyed." | |

## 4. Ludwig II's Falkenstein, Pfronten (1883–86). Proposed No. 4

| Item | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | Ludwig bought the ruin in 1883 to outdo Neuschwanstein, in Gothic style. | H: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burg_Falkenstein_(Pfronten) |
| Architects | Christian Jank (a stage-set painter, fantasy sketch 1883); then Dollmann (a small version, rejected); then Max Schultze (1884, resigned 1885); then Julius Hofmann and Eugen Drollinger. | H |
| Design | A compact castle with a great hall modelled on the Albrechtsburg. The Byzantine-church bedroom had a blue dome with gold stars, gold mosaics, the bed like an altar in an apse, and an alabaster falcon night-light. Its ceiling grew from 10 m to 14 m. No overall dimensions are published. | M: https://schloesserblog.bayern.de/geheimnisse/falkenstein-ein-projekt-ludwigs-ii |
| How far | A road and a water pipe up the mountain (1884) and a papier-mâché model. **No construction of the castle itself.** | M |
| Died | Declared unfit on 8 June 1886 (without being examined), stripped of capacity on 9 June, regency on 10 June. Found dead in Lake Starnberg on the evening of 13 June. **Say "declared insane and stripped of power", not "deposed".** Debts were over 14 million marks by 1885. Ludwig died 13 June 1886. Drollinger was on the **fourth** bedroom version and the sketch was left unfinished. | H death, M the fourth version (Blunt 1970) |
| Site now | The medieval ruin, about 1,270 m up (sources give 1,268 or 1,277), and the Hotel Blaue Burg (1897). | H |
| Drawings | King Ludwig II Museum, Herrenchiemsee: 16 designs, an oil of the bedroom, and a model ("Projekt Falkenstein", 2019). | H |
| Montage | The Byzantine palace at Graswang (1869–70, revived 1885), and the Chinese palace at Plansee (January 1886), his last project. | H: https://www.schlosslinderhof.de/englisch/park/projects.htm |

## 5. Schinkel's palace on the Acropolis, for King Otto of Greece (1834). Proposed No. 5

| Item | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | Plans sent with a letter of 9 June 1834 to Crown Prince Maximilian of Bavaria (de.wiki says the Prussian crown prince). Say "at the Bavarian court's request." | M: Philipp, *Schinkel: Late Projects* (2014) |
| Design | A palace at the empty east end of the plateau. The Parthenon, Propylaea and Erechtheion become garden set-pieces, and the colossal Athena Promachos is rebuilt. Schinkel never went to Athens. | H |
| Died | Klenze and Ludwig I opposed it, Greece was broke, and Gärtner chose a new site in late 1835. | H |
| Quote | Klenze: "a great architect's (beautiful) midsummer night's dream" | M |
| Built instead | Gärtner's palace (foundation stone 6 Feb 1836), now the **Hellenic Parliament** on Syntagma Square. The claim that its foundation stone came from the Acropolis is L. | H |
| Drawings | Berlin Kupferstichkabinett; lithographs in *Werke der höheren Baukunst* (1840s). | H |

**Paired in the same segment: Schinkel's Orianda, Crimea (1838).** The Empress Alexandra Feodorovna asked for "something like Charlottenhof." He gave her a peristyle, a Caryatid terrace over the sea, and a glass temple on a plinth. Her reply: "Warum macht er nicht noch eine kleinere Möglichkeit, statt dieser Unmöglichkeit… wir überdies zu Greisen werden möchten, ehe der Bau vollendet" ("Why doesn't he make a smaller possibility instead of this impossibility… we'd be old before it was finished") (M; Kühn 1989, p. 104). Mies van der Rohe owned the portfolio, and his Neue Nationalgalerie echoes it (M). Use neutral wording: "in Crimea."

## Stinger: L'Enfant's "President's Palace" (1791)

The plan was about four times (White House Historical Association) or five times (other sources) the size of the eventual White House; **say "several times"**. Commissioner David Stuart found the site "much too extensive", better suited to a "despotic government." (H: https://www.whitehousehistory.org/determining-where-the-white-house-must-stand)

## Checked and not used

- **Inigo Jones and Webb's Whitehall** (1638). Charles I was executed outside its only built fragment, but the previous special already covers Charles I. Stinger only.
- **Leonardo's Romorantin** (1517–19). M; the Chambord link is L.
- **Juvarra's Madrid** (1735). Too obscure.
- **Wren's Winchester.** Its shell was built, so it doesn't qualify.
