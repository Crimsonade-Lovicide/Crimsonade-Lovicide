**Target length:** 6–8 minutes.
**Facts:** every fact comes from `RESEARCH.md`. Only claims rated H go in the script as they stand; claims rated M are hedged.

**Thumbnail idea (no AI):** the 2020s cooling tower with its climbing wall and the swing ride above it, next to the 1974 Anefo photo of cows grazing in front of the reactor under construction. Text: **7 BILLION MARKS**.

## Story beats

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:20).** A chain swing rises out of a cooling tower. "This cooling tower never cooled anything. The reactor next to it cost around 7 billion marks, and it never made a single watt." Then the hook: how a finished nuclear plant ended up with a swing ride inside.
2. **The promise (about 1 min).**
   - What a breeder is, in one sentence: a reactor that makes more fuel than it burns.
   - Three countries sign up. The site is chosen near the Dutch border, close enough to "reach by bike".
   - Cows in the field, 1974.
3. **The bill (about 45 s).** The estimates as a rising chart: 310 million → 670 million → 1.5 billion → 3.2 billion → 6 billion.
4. **The fight (about 1.5 min).**
   - The farmer Josef Maas goes to court. That case becomes the Kalkar decision of 1978: the Constitutional Court rules that Parliament must decide the big questions.
   - 1977: tens of thousands march, with police checkpoints on the Rhine bridge, and a wall goes up around the site.
5. **The state turns (about 1 min).**
   - 1984: a sodium fire on the roof. 1986: Chernobyl.
   - The minister who had signed about a dozen of its permits now calls it a "hell fire".
   - The state sues the federal government and loses, but it still never signs the last two permits.
6. **Finished, and waiting (about 1 min).**
   - 1985: complete, with the sodium filled. The sodium can't be allowed to freeze, so it is kept hot and circulating for six years, at 105 million marks a year.
   - 205 plutonium fuel elements sit in storage in Hanau and Belgium, and none of them is ever loaded.
7. **The end (about 30 s).** 20 March 1991: the money stops. Each side blames the other in writing. The project's own official history closes with a line from Seneca about future generations being amazed.
8. **The twist (about 1 min).**
   - A Dutchman, from the partner country "you could reach by bike", buys the whole site for a few million. That's under a tenth of a percent of what it cost.
   - "Nuclear Water Wonderland": hotels, rides, a climbing wall on the cooling tower, and since 2009 a swing ride inside it.
8b. **Optional coda (about 30 s).** It isn't the only one: Austria finished Zwentendorf and then voted it down by 50.5%; Long Island sold Shoreham to the state for $1.
9. **Close.**
   - Return to Seneca: the descendants are amazed, just not in the way he meant.
   - The sign-off is the same as Pilots A and B.
