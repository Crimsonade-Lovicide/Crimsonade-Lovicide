# UNBUILT Hugo special: "Myths of the Unbuilt" (working title)

**Status:** first draft for review, 2 Oct 2026. No shots planned or rendered, and no credits spent.
**Runtime:** about 7:30. **Format:** 16:9, 720p (1080p upload master). **Host:** Hugo Ashby, on camera and in VO, voice Arthur. It also works as voice-over only, if Hugo is dropped on 31 Oct.
**Release target (proposed):** November 2026, timed to Reflect Orbital's launch if it happens then. It goes ahead of the palaces special.

**YouTube title (proposed):** *The Nazi "Sun Gun" Never Existed. The Space Mirror Does. (And 4 More Myths)*

Alternatives:
- *5 Famous Buildings That Were Never Built, and Never Existed*
- *Was the Statue of Liberty Made for Egypt? 5 Myths About Things Never Built*

**The spine: each myth is a little more true than the last.** No. 5 is false, No. 4 is false, No. 3 is unprovable, No. 2 is half true, and No. 1 is the myth that is coming true. Every entry ends on a verdict card.

**Built on the week-one readout:**
1. **The cold open pays off inside 10 seconds:** the sun gun, and the magazine that debunked itself. The title sting is 1.5 s.
2. **No. 5 opens on the most recognisable image on the list:** the Eiffel Tower.
3. **Searchable names:** Eiffel Tower, Taj Mahal, Gaudí, Statue of Liberty, Tesla, "Sun Gun".

**Tone.** Hugo is a fact-checker with a raised eyebrow. The jokes land on the myths, the headlines and the internet, never on the people.
- **Shah Jahan's imprisonment** is told plainly.
- **Sun gun imagery:** no Nazi symbols anywhere, and no "Hitler" in the title or thumbnail. Hillersleben is never shown as a location.
- **Labelled reconstructions:** every image of a thing that never existed is a reconstruction, labelled on screen. No fake LIFE pages, letters or documents.

**Legend**
- `SYNC`: Hugo on camera, lip-synced.
- `VO`: Hugo over b-roll.
- **Pace:** about 2.6 words per second.
- **Confidence tags:** `(M)` marks a figure that is single-sourced or approximate; the wording already hedges it. Everything else is H in `RESEARCH.md`.
- **VERDICT:** a free text card in the edit (FALSE, UNPROVABLE, HALF TRUE, COMING TRUE).

## No. 5 · "BARCELONA TURNED DOWN THE EIFFEL TOWER" · Barcelona, 1888 (0:35–1:45)

**[E1] SYNC** · Barcelona, the Passeig de Lluís Companys, the red-brick Arc de Triomf behind Hugo
> This is Barcelona. According to the internet, it could have had the Eiffel Tower. The city turned it down. Too ugly, apparently.

**[E2] VO** · a stack of modern web pages and listicles repeating the claim, the text blurred; a magnifying glass passes over them
> The story is everywhere. What it doesn't have is a source. No drawing, no letter, no proposal. Barcelona's historians have looked.

**[E3] VO** · Paris, 1887: the four iron legs of the Eiffel Tower rising from their foundations, cranes and scaffolding
> And the dates don't work. By the time Barcelona's city council took over its 1888 exhibition, the Eiffel Tower's foundations were already going in, in Paris.

**[E4] VO** · a labelled reconstruction: three unbuilt Barcelona towers drawn to scale beside the 300-metre Eiffel Tower, the tallest reaching 350 metres
> Barcelona did turn down towers. Three of them. The biggest was three hundred and fifty metres of iron, advertised as one-sixth higher than "Eiffel's famous one in Paris."

**[E5] VO** · 1887 Paris: gentlemen in top hats reading a newspaper protest in a café, shaking their heads at the rising tower
> The city that really hated the Eiffel Tower was Paris. Its artists signed a protest against "this useless and monstrous Eiffel Tower."

**[E6] SYNC** · the Arc de Triomf; VERDICT card after the line: FALSE
> So Barcelona never refused the Eiffel Tower. It refused the tower built to beat it.

## No. 4 · "THE BLACK TAJ MAHAL" · Agra, 1660s (1:45–3:00)

**[T1] SYNC** · Mehtab Bagh at dusk, across the Yamuna from the Taj Mahal, Hugo by the ruined garden wall
> Across the river from the Taj Mahal, the story goes, Shah Jahan began a second Taj, in black marble, for himself.

**[T2] VO** · a 17th-century French traveller writing by candlelight, seen from behind; then a printed page of the period, one sentence underlined
> The story comes from a French jewel merchant, Jean-Baptiste Tavernier, writing in 1676. This is everything he says: Shah Jahan "began to build his own tomb on the other side of the river, but the war with his sons interrupted his plan."

**[T3] SYNC** · Mehtab Bagh, Hugo raising an eyebrow
> Notice what's missing. He never says black.

**[T4] VO** · archaeologists in the 1990s uncovering a vast octagonal pool in a garden by the river; then the restored pool at night, the Taj reflected in it
> In the 1990s, archaeologists dug here. They found a moonlight garden, as wide as the Taj's own, and an octagonal pool, built for contemplating the Taj, and its reflection.

**[T5] VO** · Agra Fort at sunset; then the twin cenotaphs inside the Taj, the second one off-centre
> Shah Jahan spent his last years a prisoner of his son, in Agra Fort. He's buried beside his wife, slightly off-centre: the only thing in the Taj Mahal that isn't symmetrical.

**[T6] SYNC** · the pool at night, the Taj dark and inverted in the water beside Hugo; VERDICT card: FALSE
> So there was a black Taj Mahal. It's the white one, upside down, in a pond.

## No. 3 · "GAUDÍ'S MANHATTAN SKYSCRAPER" · New York, 1908 (3:00–4:10)

**[G1] SYNC** · Lower Manhattan at night, Hugo on a street corner looking up at the towers
> In 1908, Antoni Gaudí designed the tallest building in the world, for Manhattan. Or so we're told.

**[G2] VO** · a labelled reconstruction: a colossal parabolic tower of stacked domes rising over 1908 Manhattan, in the style of a 1950s lithograph
> A hotel, about three hundred and sixty metres high (M), commissioned, the story goes, by two American businessmen.

**[G3] VO** · Barcelona, 1950s: an elderly sculptor at a desk drawing tower designs by lamplight, seen from behind
> Every version of the story comes from one man: the son of Gaudí's model-maker. He published it in 1956, forty-eight years later, with drawings he made himself.

**[G4] VO** · an empty archive drawer; a bare desk; a street map of Manhattan with no mark on it
> There's no letter, no contract, no invoice and no site. Nobody has ever named the businessmen. One leading Gaudí scholar found "not one provable original drawing."

**[G5] VO** · 1936: smoke and burned papers in Gaudí's workshop at the Sagrada Família
> Others believe it. Gaudí's own papers mostly burned in 1936, so whatever could settle it may simply be gone.

**[G6] SYNC** · Lower Manhattan; VERDICT card: UNPROVABLE
> It may be Gaudí's tallest building. Or a very loyal son's tribute to his father's boss. We'll probably never know.

## No. 2 · "THE STATUE OF LIBERTY WAS MADE FOR EGYPT" · Port Said, 1867 (4:10–5:25)

**[L1] SYNC** · the deck of a New York harbour ferry, the Statue of Liberty behind Hugo
> In 2015, a headline announced that the Statue of Liberty was originally a Muslim woman. Like most headlines, it's about half right.

**[L2] VO** · Bartholdi's design for the Suez Canal: a colossal robed peasant woman holding a lamp aloft, standing at the canal mouth at Port Said, in the style of a 19th-century watercolour
> In 1867, the sculptor Frédéric Bartholdi pitched Egypt's ruler a lighthouse for the new Suez Canal: a robed peasant woman, eighty-six feet tall, holding a light aloft. "Egypt Carrying the Light to Asia."

**[L3] VO** · Port Said, 1869: a plain concrete lighthouse at the harbour mouth, ships passing
> Egypt couldn't afford it. Port Said got a concrete lighthouse instead, and later a statue of Ferdinand de Lesseps, the man who'd warned Bartholdi that Egypt couldn't pay. It came down in 1956.

**[L4] VO** · a sculptor's studio: terracotta models in a row, each one a little less like a peasant and a little more like a goddess
> Bartholdi kept the idea: a robed woman, a light held high, guarding a harbour. Historians see her evolving, sketch by sketch, into a goddess. And she grew, to a hundred and fifty-one feet.

**[L5] VO** · Bartholdi in his study in the 1880s, seen from behind, reading a newspaper
> Bartholdi denied it. The only resemblance, he said, is that "both held a light aloft." (M)

**[L6] VO** · a modern phone screen showing a viral headline, blurred; then Bartholdi's watercolour again
> And "Muslim"? No source says so. Bartholdi's drawings show a fellah, an Egyptian peasant woman. Her religion was added by a headline, in 2015.

**[L7] SYNC** · the ferry, Liberty behind; VERDICT card: HALF TRUE
> Same pose, same job, different woman.

## No. 1 · "THE NAZI SUN GUN" · 1929–1945, and now (5:25–7:00)

**[S1] SYNC** · a hilltop at night under a sky full of stars, a small telescope beside Hugo
> In July 1945, American readers learned that German scientists had planned a mirror in space, big enough to burn a city.

**[S2] VO** · Paris, June 1945: a US Army officer at a press briefing, correspondents with notebooks, seen from behind; no insignia shown
> It came from a US Army briefing in Paris: a mirror of sodium, five thousand one hundred miles up. TIME called it a "sun gun" that could "make an ocean boil or… burn up a city."

**[S3] VO** · empty archive shelves, a filing cabinet drawer sliding open on nothing
> No German drawings have ever turned up. No budget, no factory, no named scientist. At the time, one paper suggested the experts were "merely dreaming over their ersatz beer."

**[S4] VO** · 1929: a scientist at a desk, seen from behind, writing a book; then a vast mirror in orbit glinting over the Arctic, ships moving through clear water below
> The idea was real, but it wasn't a wartime weapon. The rocket pioneer Hermann Oberth published it in 1929: a mirror a hundred kilometres wide, to keep Arctic ports free of ice and save crops from frost. It could also, he added, "unfortunately," burn cities.

**[S5] VO** · a simple diagram drawn in the edit: the sun's disc, a mirror, and a forty-mile pool of light on the ground
> And LIFE magazine, in the same issue that called it a weapon for conquest, ran a sidebar explaining why it would never work. The sun isn't a point; it's a disc. From five thousand miles up, its image would be about forty miles wide. A mirror the size TIME described would deliver about a five-hundredth of ordinary sunlight.

**[S6] VO** · 1993: a thin silver disc unfolding from a spacecraft in orbit; a faint spot of light sweeping across night-time Europe below
> In 1993, Russia unfurled a twenty-metre mirror in orbit. It swept a patch of light about as bright as the full moon across Europe, before dawn.

**[S7] VO** · a modern satellite unfolding a square reflector; a soft circle of light on a dark field below
> And this year, an American company was licensed to put a mirror in orbit, to sell sunlight after dark. (M)

**[S8] SYNC** · the hilltop at night, Hugo looking up; VERDICT card: COMING TRUE
> So the sun gun never existed. The space mirror does. It's just a very expensive streetlight.
