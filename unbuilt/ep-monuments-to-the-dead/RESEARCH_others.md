# Research & fact-check: "Monuments to the Dead" (Nos. 5–2)

The Halloween countdown special, presented by Hugo Ashby. No. 1 (Willson's pyramid, Primrose Hill) is covered separately in `RESEARCH_pyramid.md`.

Confidence levels: **H** means two or more solid sources agree. **M** means one good source, or sources that differ slightly. **L** means weak or disputed. Where a primary source was read directly (the Commons Journal, Grey's *Debates*, *Parentalia*, the BnF caption, the AOC and House History pages), the table says so.

## 1. Recommendation: the final four (countdown order)

**The spine is physical scale.** Each entry is bigger than the last, ending at Willson's pyramid, which is about 900 ft (1830 version) per `RESEARCH_pyramid.md`. A second, comic spine runs underneath: how many dead each one would actually have held.

| No. | Project | Country, date | Scale | Meant to hold | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | **Washington's Tomb, US Capitol** | USA, 1799–1832 | A chamber two floors under the Rotunda | 2: George and Martha (AOC) | It is **built, real and still empty**. The only thing ever kept in it was Lincoln's pine funeral platform. The host can stand at the compass stone directly above it. |
| **4** | **Wren's mausoleum for Charles I, Windsor** | England, 1678 | Rotunda about 68 ft across and 145 ft (≈44 m) high | 1 king, possibly 3 royal coffins | Parliament voted the money on the anniversary of the execution. The King "returned the Drawings … with Orders to keep them till called for again". The site at St George's is the unluckiest tomb plot in England. |
| **3** | **Monumento al Descamisado (Evita's mausoleum), Buenos Aires** | Argentina, 1951–55 | 137 m, taller than the Statue of Liberty | 1: Eva Perón, in a silver sarcophagus in the crypt | Ground was broken, then a coup stopped the work. Her body then spent some 20 years stolen and hidden before ending up in a family vault in Recoleta. It is the strongest non-European pick, and the most dramatic turn in the episode. |
| **2** | **Boullée's Cenotaph for Newton** | France, 1784 | A sphere about 150 m across | **0**. It is a cenotaph and Newton is in Westminster Abbey | The purest image in the episode: an inverted sky, night inside during the day. It is the biggest tomb ever designed for nobody, which sets up the 5 million at No. 1. |
| 1 | *Willson's Pyramid (other researcher)* | *England, 1829–30* | *About 900 ft (1830)* | *5,167,104 coffins* | |

**Why this four works:**
- **Escalation.** The scale runs from a room, to 44 m, to 137 m, to 150 m, to about 275 m. The body count runs 2 → 1 (maybe 3) → 1 → **0** → 5 million. The drop to zero just before No. 1 is the joke that sets up the finale.
- **Variety.** USA, England, Argentina and France, then England. The eras are 1678, 1784, 1799–1832 and 1950s, then 1829. The types are an empty crypt, a royal mausoleum, a colossus and a paper sphere.
- **Places to stand.**
  - No. 5: the Capitol Crypt, and the catafalque in the Visitor Center.
  - No. 4: the quire of St George's Chapel, the Albert Memorial Chapel on the intended site, and the drawings at All Souls College, Oxford.
  - No. 3: the plaza opposite the Biblioteca Nacional, the Duarte vault in Recoleta, and the surviving Tommasi statues at San Vicente.
  - No. 2: the BnF drawings. The host can also stand at Newton's real monument in Westminster Abbey, which has a celestial globe on top, a visual rhyme with the sphere.
- **Turns in the story.**
  - No. 5: the tomb's only tenant was a platform.
  - No. 4: the King's grave was supposedly "lost". The same plot had already failed as a tomb for Henry VII, Wolsey and Henry VIII. The Albert Memorial Chapel now on it does not contain Albert.
  - No. 3: the body's odyssey, and a second unbuilt mausoleum in 1974.
  - No. 2: the day/night inversion.
- **Humour that stays on the ideas.** Parliamentary procrastination, "Motives unknown to the Publick", a building for no one, "Let it be the largest in the world". No jokes about Evita's death or what was done to her body, and none about the man who was shot (see No. 3's cautions).

**Why these beat the ones dropped** (details in section 6):
- **Julius II's tomb (Michelangelo, 1505–45).** A superb story, but its turn ("the pope isn't in it") duplicates Washington's. It is also smaller than Wren's, and extremely familiar. It is the best **substitute** if the producer wants Italy instead of the USA.
- **Robert Mills' Washington Monument pantheon.** Not a tomb. It would be a second Washington entry, and there is no body angle.
- **John Russell Pope's Lincoln pyramid (1912).** Not a tomb (Lincoln is buried in Springfield). The story is thin: Pope seemingly used the pyramid to steer the commission towards other sites.
- **Friedrich Gilly's monument to Frederick the Great (1797).** A monument, not a tomb, and visually close to Boullée. The good twist (Frederick finally buried by his dogs in 1991) runs through his coffin being hidden in WWII on Hitler's orders, which sits close to the brief's Nazi exclusion.
- **Francis Goodwin's Grand National Cemetery (1830).** The **same hill and the same year** as Willson, so it would pre-empt No. 1. Use it inside the No. 1 segment instead, or as the stinger ("and Willson wasn't even the only one").
- **Haussmann's Méry-sur-Oise necropolis (1860s–1881).** A great idea: a cemetery for all of Paris, with trains of the dead. But the sources disagree on nearly every figure (800 or 1,000 ha; 20 or 40 km; abandoned 1870 or 1881). It would be the second French entry. Its capacity is unquantified and could undercut Willson's 5 million.
- **The "Black Taj Mahal".** Almost certainly a legend. It works only as a myth-buster, not as an unbuilt project.

**Series format note.** SERIES.md (updated 27 Sep) says specials are at least five subjects, with an optional honourable mention or stinger after No. 1. These four plus the pyramid make five. Stinger candidates:
- Goodwin's rival cemetery on the same hill.
- Argentina's 1974 **Altar de la Patria**, a second unbuilt tomb for Evita, Perón and the nation's heroes (see No. 3).

**Site today.** The host can stand at the compass stone in the Crypt, directly above the empty tomb, and at the catafalque in the CVC Exhibition Hall. Filming inside the Capitol needs congressional or AOC permission.

**Striking details:**
1. It is America's most exclusive tomb. It was built for two, and the only thing ever laid in it was a pine platform.
2. There was a 10-ft hole in the Rotunda floor so the public could look down at a statue above a coffin that never arrived. It was bricked up in 1828 because of the draught.

**Cautions:**
- It is a "tomb" or "crypt", not a mausoleum building.
- The Crypt (on the tours) and Washington's Tomb (a level below) are **different rooms**. Don't let the script conflate them.

**The jinxed plot.** The site Wren was given is the building now called the **Albert Memorial Chapel**. It has failed as a tomb again and again:
- Henry VII built it as his mausoleum, then chose Westminster Abbey.
- Wolsey's tomb there was "never occupied".
- Henry VIII's monument was nearly finished and demolished in 1646. The sarcophagus Henry had taken from Wolsey now holds **Nelson** in the crypt of St Paul's.
- Charles I's mausoleum was never built.
- Victoria made it the Albert Memorial Chapel, but **Albert is buried at Frogmore**. George III, George IV and William IV lie in the royal vault beneath it.

Sources: *Parentalia* (Tomb House, 1646); Britannica, Albert Memorial Chapel; Royal Museums Greenwich (Nelson's sarcophagus "originally intended for Cardinal Wolsey"); RCT Albert site (Victoria broke with St George's burials; Frogmore). **H/M**

**Site today.** The host can stand at the quire slab in St George's Chapel, which is probably in slightly the wrong place, and at the Albert Memorial Chapel (the intended site). The drawings are in the Codrington Library, All Souls College, Oxford. St George's needs filming permission from the Dean and Canons.

**Cautions:** see the myths in section 7.

- The plaza opposite the Biblioteca Nacional. Its brutalist building, by Testa, Bullrich and Cazzaniga, stands where Evita died.
- The Duarte vault at Recoleta, one of Buenos Aires' most visited graves.
- The headless Tommasi figures at San Vicente, where Perón is also buried.

**Striking details:**
1. It was designed to be more than twice the height of the Obelisco, which is the city's own yardstick (Página/12).
2. Evita was meant to lie beneath the world's largest statue. After a 20-year journey that included a false name in Milan, she ended up in a family vault a few hundred metres from where it would have stood.

**Tone cautions (important):**
- This is recent and politically live: Peronism is current Argentine politics.
- The body's story involves real violence. Arancibia's pregnant wife was shot dead after finding the body, according to the Buenos Aires Herald. **No jokes about the body's treatment or that death.**
- Keep the humour on the monument's scale and the lift count.

**Site today.** There is no site. The host can use the BnF drawings (Richelieu or Gallica), and stand at **Newton's real monument in Westminster Abbey**, under its stone globe.

**Striking details:**
1. It was built to hold nothing: "the largest tomb ever designed for nobody".
2. Inside, it is night during the day and day at night. A lamp stands in for the sun and pinholes stand in for the stars.

**Cautions:**
- "Holes for the stars at night" is backwards. The stars are the *daytime* effect.
- "It was meant to be built in Paris" is not attested. No site is specified.

## 7. Myths and pitfalls

1. **"Charles II pocketed the £70,000."** No evidence. The Commons voted a two-month tax, the bill was ordered, the King agreed not to press for it during the French war crisis (18 Feb 1678), and no Act followed. The money was probably never raised. Script: "Parliament voted it. Nobody collected it."
2. **"Wren's mausoleum was to be in Westminster Abbey or St Paul's."** It was for Windsor, at the east end of St George's. MPs suggested St Paul's in the debate.
3. **"Grinling Gibbons designed the statues."** That is *Parentalia*'s claim, but All Souls says neither drawing is in his hand. Say "attributed to".
4. **"Charles I's grave was lost until 1813."** Half true. The *location* was forgotten, but the vault was opened in 1696 and seen. Clarendon's "couldn't find the body" is refuted in *Parentalia*.
5. **"The slab marks the vault."** It is probably slightly to the west of it (St George's archives).
6. **"Washington's tomb had a glass floor" or "the tomb was sealed in 1828."** AOC says a **10-ft open hole** in the Rotunda floor was sealed in 1828. The glass floor is a Wikipedia garble.
7. **"Washington's Tomb is the Crypt."** They are two different levels.
8. **"The Lincoln catafalque is still in Washington's Tomb."** It has been in the CVC Exhibition Hall since the CVC opened (2008).
9. **"Evita's monument was a statue of Evita."** It was a male worker, the *descamisado*. She was to lie in the crypt.
10. **"Taller than the Eiffel Tower."** Infobae says this, and it is **false**: the Eiffel Tower is about 300 m. Use the Statue of Liberty or "twice the Obelisco".
11. **"All the Tommasi statues were thrown in the Riachuelo."** Only some were. Others vanished or went to Mar del Plata, and two headless figures survive at San Vicente.
12. **Boullée's stars at night.** Backwards: pinholes give stars **by day**, and the lamp gives "day" by night.
13. **"Boullée's cenotaph was a competition entry" or "meant for a Paris site."** Not attested. It was a treatise project.
14. **Goodwin versus Willson.** Two separate Primrose Hill schemes of 1830. The £25 shares are Goodwin's.
15. **"Albert is buried in the Albert Memorial Chapel."** He is at Frogmore.

## 8. Open questions and unverified items

- Public access to Washington's tomb chamber (as opposed to the Crypt). Ask the AOC or CVC press office.
- The dimensions of Washington's tomb chamber: not found.
- The exact plot of the 1955 Evita works (Floralis Genérica plaza or opposite the Biblioteca Nacional), and whether any foundations remain.
- The height of the Descamisado statue itself (53, 60 or 61.5 m).
- Whether Mills intended Washington's remains for his pantheon.
- Beddard, "Wren's mausoleum for Charles I and the cult of the Royal Martyr", *Architectural History* 27 (1984), was **not read** (paywalled). It is the key academic source, worth getting to confirm the funding story.
